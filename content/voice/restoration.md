# Voice — Restoration

## Who is reading

Usually someone standing in water at 11pm, or a homeowner who just found mold, or a
property manager whose tenant just called. They are stressed, they are on a phone,
and they are going to call the first company that sounds like it can handle this.

They are not shopping. They are looking for relief.

## How to write

**Calm authority.** The reader is panicking; the site should not be. Short
declaratives. No exclamation marks — urgency comes from clarity, not punctuation.

**Lead with what happens next.** "Call us and a crew is at your door within 60
minutes" beats "we are your trusted restoration partner" every time. Reduce the
unknown.

**Concrete over abstract.** "We pull the baseboards, set air movers, and monitor
moisture daily until it reads dry" beats "comprehensive water mitigation services."

**Handle money early.** The unspoken question on every restoration page is *who pays
for this*. Answer it before they have to ask: insurance direct billing, we work with
your adjuster, we document everything for your claim.

**Second person.** "Your home," "your claim," "your family." Not "the property."

## Headline patterns

- `Water damage in {city}? We're on site in {time}.`
- `{Service} done right the first time — and billed straight to your insurer.`
- `{Years} years restoring {city} homes after the worst days.`

## Words that work

24/7 · on site in · we handle the insurance · certified · licensed and insured ·
documented · dry, tested, verified · no surprises · we've seen worse

## Words to avoid

- "Unfortunately", "sadly", "disaster struck" — melodrama; they know it's bad
- "State-of-the-art", "cutting-edge", "industry-leading" — meaningless filler
- "Contact us for a free quote" — in an emergency they need "call now"
- Anything cheerful. Read the room.

## Length

Homepage sections: 40–80 words. Service pages: 400–800 words — people do research
mold and insurance claims. Service-area pages: 300–500 genuinely local words, never
spun duplicates.

## Never

Invent a response time, certification, licence number or years-in-business. Every
claim traces to `client.json`. If it isn't in the file, it doesn't go on the site.
