# Voice — Combat Sports & Fitness

## Who is reading

Two very different people, and the site has to serve both:

1. **The nervous beginner.** Out of shape, intimidated, has watched UFC and assumes
   they'll be thrown to the wolves. This is the majority of your traffic and almost
   all of your revenue.
2. **The experienced athlete** shopping for a gym with real coaching and hard rounds.

Write primarily for the beginner. Signal credibility for the athlete through
specifics — coach records, lineage, affiliations — not through tone. The mistake
most gym sites make is writing entirely for person 2 and terrifying person 1.

## How to write

**Direct and confident, never macho.** The gym is tough; the copy doesn't need to
prove it. Copy that tries to sound hard reads as insecure.

**Name the fear and dismantle it.** "You will not be thrown in with a pro on day
one. Your first class is fundamentals with people who started exactly where you
are." This single move converts better than anything else on the page.

**Specifics build credibility.** "Coach Ray, 12-4 pro, black belt under Marcelo
Garcia" beats "world-class coaching staff."

**Belonging over intensity.** People stay for the room, not the workout. Community
is the retention story and it should be visible in copy and photography both.

**Short sentences. Active verbs.** Rhythm should feel like the sport.

## Headline patterns

- `Learn to fight. Or just get in the best shape of your life.`
- `{Discipline} in {city}. Start with a free week.`
- `Your first class is free. Most of our members had never trained before.`

## Words that work

free trial week · no experience needed · your first class · beginner-friendly ·
we all started somewhere · real coaching · train with people who want you to get better

## Words to avoid

- "Warriors", "beasts", "savage", "no excuses", "pain is weakness leaving the body" —
  a decade stale and it repels the beginner you need
- "Elite" as a blanket descriptor — meaningless
- Anything shaming about the reader's current fitness
- Contract language buried in fine print — say the terms plainly

## The two must-haves

1. **The trial offer above the fold**, on every page.
2. **Pricing and schedule visible.** Hiding them to force a phone call loses far
   more leads than it captures. Non-negotiable — push back if a client insists.

## Kids programs

A separate funnel with a different reader: a parent worried about discipline,
bullying, confidence and screen time. Warmer voice, safety-forward, and it deserves
its own page — never a bullet on the adult page.

## Length

Homepage sections: 30–70 words. Program pages: 400–700 words. Coach bios: 150–300
words with a real record and a real personality detail.

## Never

Invent a competition record, rank, lineage, or affiliation. In this niche those
claims are checkable by the audience and getting one wrong destroys the gym's
credibility in a community where everyone knows everyone. Every claim traces to
`client.json`.
