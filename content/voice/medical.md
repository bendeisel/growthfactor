# Voice — Medical

## Who is reading

Someone deciding whether to trust this practice with their body, or their child's,
or their parent's. Often they have already been somewhere that felt rushed or
dismissive. They are looking for competence *and* for signs they'll be treated like
a person.

## How to write

**Warm precision.** Clinical accuracy delivered in plain language. Confident without
being salesy — medicine sold like a mattress reads as untrustworthy.

**Plain language over jargon.** Name the condition the way the patient names it,
then the clinical term. "Plantar fasciitis (heel pain)" not "plantar fasciopathy."

**Answer the anxious questions.** Does it hurt? How long is recovery? Will insurance
cover it? What happens at the first visit? These are what patients actually search
for and what keeps them on the page.

**Specific credentials, no puffery.** "Board-certified in dermatology, 14 years, over
3,000 Mohs procedures" beats "one of the region's leading skin experts."

**Respect intelligence.** Patients read. Long-form procedure content performs well
here in a way it doesn't in other niches.

## Headline patterns

- `{Specialty} care in {city}, without the wait.`
- `Board-certified {specialty}. Same-week appointments.`
- `Understanding {condition} — and what we can do about it.`

## Words that work

board-certified · same-week · we'll explain everything · your options · evidence-based ·
in-network with · unhurried · you'll leave knowing

## Words to avoid

- "Best", "top-rated", "#1", "leading" — comparative superiority claims are a state
  board advertising problem, not just bad writing
- "Guaranteed results", "painless", "permanent" — outcome guarantees, do not use
- "Miracle", "breakthrough", "revolutionary"
- "Cheap", "discount" — price-competes away the trust you're building

## Compliance — hard rules

1. **No outcome guarantees.** Ever. Not implied, not hedged.
2. **No comparative superiority claims** without documented substantiation.
3. **No PHI collected** in a standard web form. Route to HIPAA-compliant intake.
4. **Testimonials** must have consent on file and clear the relevant state medical
   board advertising rules — these vary by state and by specialty.
5. **Before/after imagery** requires documented patient consent and, in most states,
   a disclaimer that results vary.
6. **WCAG 2.2 AA is a legal exposure area** in this niche, not a nicety.

When unsure whether a claim is permissible, flag it for the client's compliance
review rather than publishing it. This is one of the few places in the pipeline
where the correct move is to slow down.

## Length

Homepage sections: 50–100 words. Procedure pages: 800–1500 words — patients research
deeply and these pages rank. Provider bios: 200–400 words with a genuine human
detail, not just credentials.

## Never

Invent a credential, affiliation, procedure volume, or insurance acceptance. Every
claim traces to `client.json`.
