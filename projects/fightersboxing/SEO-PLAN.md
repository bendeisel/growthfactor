# Fighters Boxing Gym: SEO plan

Goal Ben set: beat the current Fighters Nashville site by a wide margin,
be strong for both AI search and local search, and double the leads.

Everything in Part 1 is measured from the client's own WordPress export
and from our build, not assumed. Numbers you can check are marked.

---

## Part 1: Where things actually stand

### The current site is running on autopilot

The live site has the All in One SEO plugin installed. Across all 34 pages
and posts:

| Field | Filled in |
| --- | --- |
| SEO title | **0 of 34** |
| Meta description | **0 of 34** |
| Target keywords | **0 of 34** (all empty `a:0:{}`) |

Every title and description on the live site today is whatever the plugin
generated by default. Nobody has ever set one deliberately. That is not a
small gap, it is the single cheapest ranking and click-through win
available, and it is currently at zero.

### What the new build already fixes

| | Current live site | New build |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Unique page titles | Plugin defaults | 25 of 25, written per page |
| Meta descriptions | None | 25 of 25 |
| Business schema | None found | `SportsActivityLocation` with full address, geo, phone, on every page |
| FAQ schema | None | `FAQPage`, 22 questions |
| Blog post schema | None | `BlogPosting` on all 10 posts |
| Sitemap | Plugin default | Generated, 25 URLs with lastmod |
| Page count | 16 pages, 10 posts | 25 pages, all indexable |

One defect found and fixed while writing this: our own `sitemap.xml` was a
hand-written stub listing only the homepage. It was correct the day it was
written, when the site was one page, and quietly wrong for every page
added since. It now generates from the pages themselves, so it cannot
drift again.

### The assets nobody is using

This is the most important section in this document.

Fighters has authority signals that almost no competing gym in Nashville
can match, and the current site mentions them almost nowhere:

1. **Dr. Christy Halbert: 2012 Olympic Coach for Team USA, and an
   International Women's Boxing Hall of Fame inductee.** She has a PhD and
   an academic background in sociology. This is a nationally significant
   credential sitting in a single paragraph in a blockquote.
2. **Fighters Boxing Club is a program of Boxing Resource Center, a
   501(c)(3) nonprofit founded in 2001.** Twenty five years of continuous
   operation, with an educational mission. Found buried in one blog post.
3. **Sena Agbeko, a professional boxer, is on the coaching staff.**
4. **USA Boxing certified coaches**, referees and judges among them.

Search engines and AI assistants both weight expertise, experience,
authority and trust. An Olympic coach running a 25 year old nonprofit is
about as strong as that gets for a local gym, and today a search engine
would struggle to learn it from the site.

**This is where the "10x" actually comes from.** Not from publishing more
blog posts. From making verifiable, unusual, checkable facts prominent and
machine readable.

## Part 2: Answering Ben's question about the blogs

### Do the 10 ported posts help?

Modestly, and they are worth keeping, but they are not a growth engine.

**Why keep them:** they are real writing, they carry whatever backlinks and
history they already earned, they cost nothing to keep, and removing URLs
that already exist is a net loss. Keeping their exact original URLs means
none of that history breaks.

**Why they are not the engine:** they are broad-topic posts ("Jake Paul vs
Mike Tyson", "8 Nasty Career Traps for Pro Boxers") that attract readers
anywhere in the world. A person in Ohio reading about Jake Paul will never
walk into a gym on 42nd Ave N. They are traffic, not leads.

### Should we build other blogs and point backlinks at them?

If this means **building separate sites we own and linking them back to the
main site**: no. That is a private blog network. It is an explicit
violation of Google's link spam policies, and the downside is not "it
stops working", it is a manual action against the money site. For a
business whose entire lead flow depends on local search, that risk is
completely out of proportion to the upside.

If this means **writing better, more targeted posts on the main site and
linking them properly**: yes, absolutely, and that is the content plan in
Part 5.

The link building that does work for a gym like this one is in Part 6, and
Fighters is unusually well placed for it because of the nonprofit and the
Olympic connection.

## Part 3: Local SEO, the biggest lever

For a gym, local search drives the leads. Realistically this is worth more
than everything else in this document combined, and most of it happens off
the website.

### Google Business Profile

This is the highest-value asset in local search, ahead of the website.

- **Claim and fully complete it.** Every field. Categories, hours,
  service areas, attributes, description, opening date.
- **Primary category: "Boxing gym".** Secondary categories for the other
  real services (gym, personal trainer, youth organization if the
  nonprofit programming qualifies).
- **Post weekly.** Class schedules, competition results, coach news. GBP
  posts are one of the few things that visibly move local rankings.
- **Photos, constantly.** Geo-tagged, real, recent. Gyms with active photo
  streams outperform ones with a 2019 stock shot.
- **Products/Services entries** for each class, mirroring the site's class
  pages word for word so the entity is consistent.

### Reviews, which are the real ranking factor

Review count and velocity are among the strongest local ranking signals,
and they are also the single biggest conversion factor for a gym.

- Ask every member at a natural moment (after a first class, after a
  competition, at renewal). A simple text with a direct review link
  converts far better than a poster on the wall.
- Aim for a steady drip, not a burst. Twenty reviews over ten months beats
  twenty in one week, which looks manipulated.
- Reply to every single one, including the bad ones, in the owner voice.
- **Never buy or incentivize reviews.** Beyond the policy violation, one
  filtered review wave can cost the profile its standing.

Note for the site: we deliberately do not display a star rating or review
count anywhere yet, because inventing one is not an option and I have no
verified figure. Once the real Google rating exists, it belongs on the
site, with `AggregateRating` schema pointing at the real number.

### NAP consistency

The name, address and phone must be byte-identical everywhere: site,
Google, Apple Maps, Bing Places, Yelp, Facebook, and every directory.

Current canonical version, used across the new build:

```
Fighters Boxing Gym
405 42nd Ave N, Nashville, TN 37209
629-289-2988
support@fightersnashville.com
```

One thing to settle before launch: the blog posts call it **"Fighters
Boxing Club"** and reference **"Boxing Resource Center"** as the parent
nonprofit. The site says **"Fighters Boxing Gym"**. Pick the canonical
public name and use it consistently, then note the other two as the legal
and program names in the About and schema. Inconsistent naming splits the
entity in exactly the systems we are trying to convince.

### Local citations

Consistent listings on the sites that matter: Apple Maps, Bing Places,
Yelp, Nextdoor, Facebook, plus boxing-specific and Nashville-specific
directories. This is a one-time cleanup with an annual re-check, not an
ongoing programme.

## Part 4: AI SEO

AI assistants answer questions by pulling from sources they can parse,
trust, and quote. Optimizing for that overlaps heavily with classic SEO
but weights a few things differently.

### Be answerable

AI answers favour content that states a fact plainly and completely in one
place, rather than burying it in marketing prose. "Fighters Boxing Gym
offers beginner boxing classes at 6am, 7am, and 5:45pm on weekdays at 405
42nd Ave N in Nashville" is quotable. "Come experience the Fighters
difference" is not.

Practical version: every page should answer its own question in the first
hundred words, in complete sentences, before any persuasion starts.

### Be structured

Already in the build, and worth extending:

| Schema | Status | Value |
| --- | --- | --- |
| `SportsActivityLocation` | Done, all pages | The core business entity |
| `FAQPage` | Done, 22 Q&As | Directly feeds answer engines |
| `BlogPosting` | Done, 10 posts | Article attribution |
| `Person` for each coach | **To add** | Carries the Olympic credential as data |
| `Course` for each class | **To add** | Makes each class a distinct entity |
| `Event` for the schedule | **To add** | Class times become machine readable |
| `AggregateRating` | Blocked | Needs a real review count first |
| `Organization` with `nonprofitStatus` | **To add** | The 501(c)(3) as a fact |

The `Person` schema for Christy Halbert is the highest-value single
addition on this list. It turns "we have a good coach" into a structured,
checkable claim tied to Team USA and a hall of fame.

### Be consistent across the web

AI systems cross-check. The gym's name, address, coach credentials and
class list should say the same thing on the site, on Google, on Facebook,
on Instagram, and in any press. Contradictions get resolved by ignoring
the source, and the site is the source we control.

### Be citable

Original, specific, first-hand material is what gets quoted: real class
schedules, real coach credentials, real competition results, real answers
to real beginner questions. Generic advice that exists on a thousand other
sites gets ignored in favour of the bigger site that also has it.

## Part 5: The content plan

The rule: **every page must be able to convert a Nashville resident.**
National-interest content is a bonus, never the goal.

### Tier 1: the money pages (already built, need tuning)

The class pages, the schedule, coaches, what to expect, contact. These are
what people search before joining. They are built. What they still need:

- The first hundred words of each rewritten to answer the page's question
  directly (see "Be answerable").
- `Course` schema per class.
- Coach photos (see the note at the end).
- Internal links from every blog post into the relevant class page. Right
  now the posts are dead ends, which wastes the only internal link equity
  the blog has.

### Tier 2: local intent pages, the real growth

These do not exist yet and are where new local traffic comes from. Each
targets a search a Nashville person actually types:

1. Boxing gym in Nashville (the main commercial term)
2. Boxing classes for beginners in Nashville
3. Youth boxing in Nashville / boxing for kids
4. Women's boxing in Nashville
5. Boxing gym near West Nashville / The Nations / Sylvan Park (real
   neighbourhoods near 42nd Ave N)
6. Amateur boxing and competition training in Nashville
7. Private boxing lessons Nashville
8. Boxing for fitness vs boxing for competition

These are pages, not blog posts, and each should be genuinely specific to
Fighters rather than a keyword template. Several are close to what already
exists and could be expansions rather than new pages.

### Tier 3: the blog, repositioned

Keep the 10 posts. Going forward, write for the person deciding whether to
walk in:

- What to expect on your first day (already a page, and it is the highest
  intent question there is)
- What to bring, what gear to buy, what it costs
- How the competition pathway works at Fighters specifically
- Coach profiles and competition results as they happen
- The nonprofit's story and youth programme outcomes

Two genuinely specific posts a month beats eight generic ones. The generic
ones compete with the entire internet; "how our competition team works"
competes with nobody.

## Part 6: Links, the legitimate kind

Fighters has better link opportunities than a typical gym, because of the
nonprofit and the Olympic connection.

- **The 501(c)(3) angle.** Nonprofit directories, community foundation
  listings, grant announcements, youth programme partners. These are real,
  earned, high-trust links.
- **Christy Halbert's own footprint.** An Olympic coach and hall of fame
  inductee has interviews, panels, and academic citations. Every one of
  those should point at the gym.
- **Local press.** Nashville outlets cover competition results, youth
  programmes, and gyms with a story. A 25 year old nonprofit run by an
  Olympic coach is a story.
- **USA Boxing and sanctioning bodies.** Club listings and event pages.
- **Local partnerships.** Schools, physical therapists, other gyms,
  community organizations.
- **Competition results.** Every amateur result that gets published
  somewhere is a citation opportunity.

None of this is fast, and all of it compounds. It is also the only kind
that cannot be taken away in an algorithm update.

## Part 7: Measurement, and an honest word on the targets

### Set up first, before launch

- Google Search Console, verified, sitemap submitted.
- Google Analytics 4, with conversions defined as the lead form submit and
  the phone tap, not as pageviews.
- Call tracking, or at minimum a consistent "how did you hear about us"
  question at signup.
- A baseline snapshot of current rankings, traffic and lead volume on the
  day before launch. Without this, none of the rest is provable.

### What to watch

| Metric | Why |
| --- | --- |
| Leads per month | The only number that pays anyone |
| Lead conversion rate | Traffic times conversion equals leads. Both are levers |
| GBP calls, direction requests, profile views | Local intent, the highest value traffic there is |
| Review count and average | Ranking factor and conversion factor at once |
| Rankings for the Tier 2 terms | Whether the new pages are landing |
| Branded vs non-branded search | Whether the gym is reaching new people or just the ones who knew it |

### On "10x the SEO" and "double the leads"

Straight answer: nobody can promise a multiplier, and anyone who does is
selling something. What I can say specifically:

- The current site has **zero** deliberately written titles and
  descriptions. Going from nothing to complete is a genuinely large
  improvement, and it is the easiest part.
- The gym's authority assets are real, unusual, and currently invisible.
  Making them prominent is high leverage and low risk.
- **Doubling leads probably comes more from conversion than traffic.** The
  new site has a lead popup on every CTA, a real schedule, real coaches
  and a clear first-visit path. If the current site converts at 1% and the
  new one converts at 2%, leads double on identical traffic. That is a far
  shorter path than doubling traffic, and it is mostly already built.
- Local rankings also depend on proximity to the searcher and on review
  volume, neither of which the website controls. The Google Business
  Profile and review work in Part 3 is not optional garnish, it is
  probably the largest single lever available.

Realistic shape: measurable movement in titles, click-through and
indexation within weeks of launch. Local ranking movement over two to
four months, driven mostly by GBP and reviews. Content-driven growth from
the Tier 2 pages over three to six months.
