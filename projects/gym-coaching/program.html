What the numbers say

Four findings that set the curriculum

$361

One campaign is leaking more than the coaching costs. Kids Martial Arts bought 37 leads for $599.14. At the cohort's own best rate of $6.42, those same 37 leads cost $237.54. That is $361.60 a month inside a single campaign, in one gym, this month.

32.9%

The worst CPL has the best creative, so the offer is the problem. Kids Martial Arts stops more scrolls than anything else on the board and still buys the most expensive lead. Changing the video will not fix it.

The opposite case is right there too: Fighters' $80/m for a 3 month commitment hooks 13.99% and pays $15.94. That one really is the creative. Telling those two apart is module 4.

279

The leads are already arriving and nobody has timed the reply. 279 people asked about training last month across three gyms. Response time is not measured anywhere. That makes the follow-up bot the cheapest available win, because it costs nothing per lead, which is why it is module 2 and not module 6.

107/45

At Fighters, leads outnumber landing page views. 107 leads against 45 page views means they arrive through Meta instant forms and never touch the website. Worth saying plainly for a company that sells websites: on these campaigns the site is not in the ad path.

Instant-form leads are also lower intent, which raises the value of a fast reply rather than lowering it. Modules 2 and 5 are one system.

$297 per month

The offer

What it buys

  • Onboarding, 90 minutes one to one. Connect the accounts, pull the baseline, build one ad together while they watch.
  • Weekly call, 60 minutes. Whole cohort, same time every week. Hot seats and account reviews.
  • Eight modules. Guided builds they run on their own account, async.
  • Four systems they own. Follow-up bot, front desk agent, review engine, ad build. In their GHL, their ad account.
  • Monthly numbers. The baseline re-pulled and compared. Spend, leads, CPL per campaign, what moved.
  • A channel for questions between calls.

What it does not

  • We do not run the ads. They do. We build the first one with them.
  • We do not answer the leads. Their bot does, and they own it.
  • Website is separate. $100 a month, up to 10 pages.
  • AEO, GEO and technical SEO are quoted at $200 to $500. Local and basic on-page SEO are already in the website plan.
  • Ad spend is theirs, paid to the platform directly. It never runs through us.

The price only works if the human time is bounded. Onboarding is 90 minutes once per member. The weekly call is 60 minutes and does not get longer as the cohort grows. Everything else is async and reusable. That is four hours of live time a month regardless of headcount. Twenty members is $5,940 a month against those four hours plus onboarding spread across the quarter. The failure mode is the same one that kills $100 websites: if an answer is not in a module yet, answering it on the weekly call is what turns it into one.

Four months in arrears, roughly $1,188 outstanding, and a finished homepage rebuild sitting unshipped.

Recommendation, once, then it is a business call and not a technical one: do not ship the rebuild and do not add them to the cohort until the balance is current. The build stays on the branch either way and stays ready.

Answer before the sales page

Open items

  1. Contract length and cohort cap.Month to month is easier to sell and easier to churn out of in week 3, before anything has worked. Three months matches how long the modules take to pay off. Separately, the weekly call stops working somewhere past 15 to 20 live participants, so pick the number before it arrives. Neither blocks recording.
  2. AdKit access for members.Checked against the live AdKit contract on 3 Sep. There is a public brief share link and there are workspaces, but no client seat or affiliate program surfaced in the help catalog. All five projects sit in one workspace Ben owns, which is enough to produce every member's monthly report today. The revenue share is unverified, so do not promise a seat yet. Three questions to put to AdKit are in the project README.
  3. Google Ads is missing from the baseline.Connected on Nashville MMA and Fuel Fortress, not pulled. Add it before the first monthly review or the report understates spend and flatters the CPL.
  4. Nothing has measured speed to lead.Module 2 rests on response time being the cheapest win. That is sound in general and unmeasured here. The first member to switch the bot on should record their before number, even roughly, so the module gets a real result attached to it.
