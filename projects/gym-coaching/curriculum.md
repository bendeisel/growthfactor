# The modules

Eight guided builds. Every module ends with something switched on in the
member's own account, not with a quiz. Ordered by money, not by difficulty:
module 2 is the follow-up bot because 279 leads a month are already arriving
and nothing is catching them.

**Scripted in full below:** 01 and 02. Record those first, they unblock the
three paying members.
**Outlined as beats:** 03 to 08. Enough to record from, not yet word for word.

## Recording standard

- **Screen recording of the real account**, member's own if they consent,
  otherwise Nashville MMA's. No slides, no stock footage, no talking head for
  more than the cold open.
- **8 to 12 minutes.** Anything longer is two modules.
- **One deliverable per module**, stated in the first 30 seconds and shown
  working in the last 60.
- **Show the failure.** Every module includes the thing that goes wrong, because
  that is the part that stops a support message.
- **No music.** No intro animation. Cold open on the problem.

# Module 01 — Know your number

**Runtime:** 9 minutes
**Deliverable:** the member can say their blended CPL out loud, per campaign,
without opening a spreadsheet.

## Script

**[00:00] Cold open, on screen: the campaign list, unsorted.**

"This is a real gym's ad account. Sixteen campaigns. Thirteen of them spent
nothing last month. I want you to find the one that is wasting the most money.
Take a second. You cannot, and neither can the owner, and that is the whole
problem. Let's fix it in nine minutes."

**[00:40] What we are actually measuring.**

"Three numbers. Spend, leads, and spend divided by leads. That third one is cost
per lead, CPL, and it is the only ad number that matters to you right now. Not
impressions, not reach, not engagement. If someone shows you a screenshot with
reach on it, they are hiding the CPL."

**[01:30] Screen: pull the report.**

Walk the exact steps. Project, then results, then campaigns, then last 30 days.
Say out loud that the window is 30 days and it always will be, because comparing
a 7-day number to a 30-day number is how people convince themselves something
improved.

**[03:15] Read a real row.**

On screen: Jiu-Jitsu Ads. $600.73 spent, 60 leads, $10.01 each.

"Six hundred dollars, sixty leads, ten dollars a lead. Now the one underneath
it. Kids Martial Arts. Five hundred and ninety-nine dollars, thirty-seven leads,
sixteen nineteen each. Almost the same money. Twenty-three fewer leads."

**[04:30] The comparison that does the work.**

"Here is another gym, twenty minutes up the road, same sport. Six dollars and
forty-two cents a lead. So inside one operator, in one city, in one month, the
same lead costs between six dollars and sixteen dollars. Two and a half times.
Nobody's ad account is broken. The gap is just invisible until you pull this
report."

**[05:45] Do the subtraction on screen.**

"Thirty-seven leads at six forty-two is two hundred thirty-seven dollars. They
paid five ninety-nine. That campaign is leaking three hundred sixty-one dollars
a month. Write that number down, because in module 4 we go get it."

**[06:40] The dormant campaigns.**

"Thirteen campaigns at zero dollars. Muay Thai Ad from September 2024. New Years
Special. Spring Sale. Summer Sale. None of them cost anything. All of them cost
you every time you open this account and have to find the live one. Module 3."

**[07:30] Your homework, on screen as a filled-in example.**

"Pull your own. Write down four things: total spend, total leads, blended CPL,
and your worst campaign by CPL. Four numbers. Post them in the channel before
the call. Not a screenshot. The four numbers."

**[08:20] Close.**

"You cannot improve a number you have never said out loud. Next module is the
one that makes you money without spending another dollar on ads."

# Module 02 — Speed to lead

**Runtime:** 12 minutes
**Deliverable:** a live GHL Conversation AI bot that answers inbound leads and
books a trial, tested by the member on their own phone.

## Script

**[00:00] Cold open.**

"Across three gyms, two hundred and seventy-nine people asked about training
last month. I have no idea how fast any of them got a reply, and neither does
anyone else. That is not a criticism. It is just not measured anywhere. Today we
make it not matter, because the reply happens in under a minute whether anyone
is awake or not."

**[00:50] Why this beats better ads.**

"Module 1 was about paying less per lead. This one is about losing fewer of the
leads you already bought. It costs nothing per lead. If you only do one module
this month, do this one."

**[01:30] What the bot is and is not.**

"It answers, it qualifies, it books. It does not sell, it does not negotiate
price, and it does not pretend to be you. Six rules go in the prompt and they
are all rules about what it must not do."

Walk the six hard rules on screen from
`projects/reviewhappy/conversation-ai-prompt.md`.

**[03:00] Screen: build it.**

Conversation AI, new bot. Paste the prompt. Point at each of the six rules as it
lands in the box so the member sees the prompt is not magic, it is instructions.

**[05:30] The knowledge base.**

"Twelve slots. This is the only part that is different for your gym. Class
times, trial offer, price, address, parking, what to wear, kids ages, everything
someone asks at 9pm. Fill these twelve and the bot stops guessing."

Fill three of the twelve live, at real speed, so the member sees it is a
15-minute job and not a project.

**[07:45] Suggestive mode, not auto-pilot.**

"Turn it on in suggestive mode. It drafts, you approve. Leave it there for two
weeks and read every draft. You are not testing the bot, you are finding the
twelve things you forgot to put in the knowledge base. Then flip it live."

**[09:00] Show the failure.**

Ask the bot something not in the knowledge base. Let it answer badly on camera.

"That is what a missing knowledge base slot looks like. It did not break, it
guessed. This is exactly why two weeks in suggestive mode."

**[10:30] Test it on yourself.**

Text the number from a personal phone. Full round trip on screen, ending in a
booked slot on the calendar.

**[11:30] Close.**

"Do not tell me it works. Text it yourself, book yourself in, and screenshot the
calendar. That is the deliverable. Next module we clean the account so you can
actually see what you are doing."

# Module 03 — Clean the account

**Runtime:** 8 minutes
**Deliverable:** dormant campaigns archived, duplicate names resolved, a naming
convention in place.

Beats:
- Cold open on 13 dormant campaigns and two campaigns with the identical name.
- Archiving is not deleting. Learning stays with the account.
- The duplicate `$80/m for a 3 month commitment` at Fighters: one dormant, one
  spending $573. Show how long it takes to work out which is which.
- Naming convention: `Audience | Offer | Month`. Anything older than 90 days and
  at zero spend gets archived.
- Turn off Messenger as a destination. 17 conversations across three gyms at $73
  to $573 each is not a channel.
- Deliverable: a screenshot of the account showing only live campaigns.

# Module 04 — Two ways an ad fails

**Runtime:** 11 minutes
**Deliverable:** the member correctly diagnoses their own worst campaign as
either creative or offer, and writes the fix.

Beats:
- Hook rate is how many people did not scroll past. CPL is how many acted.
- **Case A, the offer is wrong.** Kids Martial Arts: best hook on the board at
  32.87%, worst CPL at $16.19. People stopped and then did not want it. Changing
  the video will not help. This is the counter-intuitive one, spend the time.
- **Case B, the creative is wrong.** `$80/m for a 3 month commitment`: hook
  13.99%, CPL $15.94. Nobody stopped. Change the video.
- The two-by-two: high hook plus high CPL is an offer problem, low hook plus
  high CPL is a creative problem, and low hook plus low CPL means a small
  audience that loves you.
- Note the naming tell: the two worst CPLs in the cohort are both campaigns
  named after a price. The best is named after the sport.
- Deliverable: which case is my worst campaign, and the one thing I am changing.

# Module 05 — Build the ad

**Runtime:** 12 minutes
**Deliverable:** one campaign built as a draft in the member's own AdKit project.

Beats:
- Built with them on the onboarding call first, then rebuilt alone here.
- Drafts by default. Nothing publishes without the member pressing publish.
- Objective, audience, budget, creative, lead form, in that order.
- The lead form is where the leads actually come from: Fighters got 107 leads
  against 45 landing page views, so almost all of it is instant form.
- Because it is instant form, the follow-up bot from module 2 is doing the
  qualifying that a landing page would have done. The two modules are one system.
- Deliverable: a draft campaign, reviewed on the weekly call before it goes live.

# Module 06 — The front desk

**Runtime:** 9 minutes
**Deliverable:** the agent live on the site and on social DMs.

Beats:
- Same knowledge base as module 2, second surface. Fill it once, use it twice.
- Schedule, price, trial, location, kids ages, what to bring.
- Handoff rule: complaint, injury, or billing goes to a human immediately and
  notifies the owner.
- Show it answering a class-time question correctly, then show it handing off.
- Deliverable: agent live, one handoff tested.

# Module 07 — Reviews without breaking the law

**Runtime:** 11 minutes
**Deliverable:** the review engine live, first ask sent.

Beats:
- Seven custom fields, not tags. Fields survive bulk tag operations.
- The exit condition is "left a review", any star rating. Never "left a good
  review". That distinction is review gating and it is the whole module.
- The bot never drafts review text. That is an AI-generated review under
  16 CFR 465.
- Never withhold or delay the link based on sentiment.
- Three lifetime asks, 30 days apart, each tied to a real service event. This
  protects the shared 10DLC campaign from complaint-driven filtering.
- Show the smart list definition and why `gave_google_review = No` is the
  starting state.
- Deliverable: fields created, smart list built, one ask sent.

# Module 08 — The monthly loop

**Runtime:** 8 minutes
**Deliverable:** a standing 30-minute monthly appointment with the member's own
numbers in front of them.

Beats:
- Re-pull module 1's report on the same day every month. Same window, always 30
  days.
- Four numbers again: spend, leads, blended CPL, worst campaign.
- Compare to last month, not to a goal. Direction beats target.
- One change per month. Two changes and you learn nothing from either.
- What to bring to the weekly call when the number went the wrong way.
- Deliverable: the appointment exists in the calendar, recurring.
