Review Happy · Build Spec v1

The reviews agent, built once and cloned

A Google-only review engine on GoHighLevel: one master sub-account, one snapshot, twelve knowledge-base slots per client. This is the architecture, the state machine, and the one problem that will otherwise quietly break it.

On using Claude as the knowledge base

It can't be. The GHL Conversation AI bot answers a text message in real time from its own knowledge base — nothing external is in that loop. So Claude is the tool that writes the knowledge base, not the thing that serves it. That's still the bigger half of the work: slots 5–9 are the slow ones, and Claude drafts them from the client's website and review history in minutes. Then you paste and snapshot.

02 The problem that breaks the whole design

A Google review does not tell you who wrote it.

Your super-agent is supposed to check daily for new reviews and flip gave_google_review on the contact who left one. Here's the catch: a Google review carries a display name and a star rating. No email. No phone. Nothing that joins to a CRM record — and the display name is a Google account name, so it shows up as "Mike S.", "mssmith82", or a nickname that matches nothing in the database.

Matching names against your whole contact list produces false positives, and a false positive is expensive in both directions: you either stop asking someone who never reviewed, or you keep texting someone who already did.

The fix is to shrink the pool before you match. A GHL trigger link on every review request logs the click, so at any moment you have a short list of people who opened the review page in the last 14 days. Matching eight new reviewer names against twenty candidates is reliable. Against four thousand contacts it is not.

One MCP caveat that shapes this: HighLevel's official MCP server exposes 21 tools — Contacts, Conversations, Calendars, Opportunities, Payments, Locations, and Custom Fields. Reviews are not among them. So Claude can write the flag but can't read the reviews through the official server. For the read side you go at the API v2 reviews endpoint directly.

There are community GHL MCP servers advertising 250–560 tools including reputation endpoints. Think hard before pointing one at client data: it takes a Private Integration Token with full CRM access, and that's every client's contact database sitting behind someone else's unaudited code. A thin function you own, hitting the documented reviews endpoint, is a better trade.

03 Five corrections to the spec

Four small, one that carries real legal exposure.

Legal

The exit condition is "left a review", not "left a good review"

You described the loop as continuing until the customer gives a good review. Make it any review, any star count — a 2-star flips the flag and exits the loop exactly like a 5-star does.

The distinction is the whole ballgame. Re-asking people who didn't review is normal and fine. Re-asking people who reviewed negatively is review gating: a Google policy violation and review suppression under the FTC's rule, which carries civil penalties per violation. Same workflow, one field definition apart.

Legal

The bot may never draft review text

"The AI follows up and gets a good review" is fine if the AI is answering questions and resending a link. It is not fine if the AI writes wording for the customer to paste — that is an AI-generated review, named explicitly in the FTC rule. The prompt in the repo hard-codes the refusal and the deflection line.

Logic

The flag inverts the way you wrote it

You said "once the field goes to no, they no longer go into any of the workflows." No is the starting state — everyone begins there and stays in the loop. Yes is the exit. Worth being pedantic about because you'll be building the filter conditions from this sentence.

Keep

Don't delete the opportunity — move it to a won stage

You're right that fields beat tags: tags get bulk-cleared and keep no history. But deleting the opportunity on conversion destroys the only number that renews the contract. Move it to a Reviewed won-stage instead; the count in that stage is the monthly client report. The smart-list filter already removes them from active workflows, so nothing needs deleting.

Keep

Cap the loop at three asks

"Keep trying until they do" needs a ceiling: three lifetime asks, minimum 30 days apart, each tied to a real new service event. Never a standalone nag.

This isn't squeamishness, it's infrastructure. Repeated unsolicited review texts draw spam complaints, complaint rates trigger carrier filtering, and sustained filtering gets a 10DLC campaign suspended. If your clients share a campaign, one over-aggressive account takes SMS down for everybody on it.

04 State machine

Seven fields, three workflows, two exits.

StateConditionWhat fires
Eligible gave_google_review = No, review_opt_out = No, review_asked_count < 3, last ask > 30 days ago Sits in the smart list, waiting on a service event.
Asked Service event fires, vertical delay elapses One SMS with the trigger link. review_asked_count +1, review_last_asked_date stamped.
Clicked Trigger link opened review_link_clicked = Yes. Enters the 14-day matching pool.
Engaged Customer replies to the SMS Conversation AI takes the thread. Answers questions, resends the link, never drafts review copy.
Reviewed (exit) Self-reported, name-matched, or manually confirmed gave_google_review = Yes + review_confirmation_source. Bot disabled for the contact, opportunity → Reviewed, drops out of the smart list.
Opted out (exit) Customer asks not to be asked again review_opt_out = Yes. Permanent, and separate from a global SMS unsubscribe.
Needs owner Bot detects a complaint review_needs_owner = Yes, owner notified, bot stops. Link stays available — never withheld.
Dormant review_asked_count = 3 Nothing. Permanently out of the loop.

06 Cloning to the next account

Target: under an hour, once the master snapshot exists.

Your instinct was right and GHL made it native in 2026: create a snapshot with the Conversation AI category selected and the bot, its prompt, the knowledge base, FAQs, and tone-of-voice all travel together. One gotcha — if the target sub-account already has a bot with the same name, GHL won't auto-merge it and you resolve the conflict by hand.

Per client, only three things are genuinely new: connect their Google Business Profile, fill the 12 knowledge-base slots, and pick the vertical's timing. Everything else rides in on the snapshot.

Two settings worth being deliberate about. Start every account with Reviews AI in Suggestive mode, not Auto-Pilot — the first ten drafts tell you whether the tone slot is right, and a bad automated reply to a 1-star review is public and permanent. Consider leaving 1- and 2-star replies manual for good. And send a test to your own phone before enabling the service trigger; the failure mode otherwise is a misconfigured delay texting a client's entire customer list at once.

For non-GHL clients, their CRM fires a Zap into GHL and the same workflows run untouched. Since you earn on that referral, put a one-line disclosure in the proposal — it covers the FTC material-connection requirement and costs you nothing.
