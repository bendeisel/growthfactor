# 14.1 The daily ten minutes

**Module:** 14 The Operating Rhythm
**Video:** ~5 min
**Needs first:** the whole course
**You finish with:** a habit that fits in the gap before your first class

## Why this matters

Everything you built runs itself overnight. Your job in the morning is to
check it did, and to pick up the handful of things it could not.

Ten minutes. On your phone. Before your first class.

## The routine

### 1. Needs a human, three minutes

Open the smart list from 02.2.

Every person on it is waiting for you. Deal with them first, before anything
else in your day.

Usually zero to three. Reply, then remove `staff-handling` when you are done.
See 09.11.

### 2. Who is coming in, two minutes

Today's calendar. Read the names and note anything relevant: a kid, a bad
knee mentioned in the conversation, a referral from a member.

Tell whichever coach is on.

This is the smallest thing in the routine and it is the one members notice.
Being greeted by name with the right context, on a first visit, is worth more
than anything else you will do today.

### 3. Yesterday's attendance, two minutes

Mark who showed and who did not, unless a check-in integration does it. See
04.3.

Without this, `no-show-recovery` never fires and your reporting is fiction.

### 4. The board, two minutes

Open Opportunities and look for stuck cards. See 04.5.

- Anything in Showed from more than a week ago
- Anything in Booked with a date in the past
- Anything in New Lead older than a day, which should be impossible

### 5. Never contacted, one minute

Open the list. It should be empty.

**If it is not empty, that is an alarm, not a chore.** Leads are arriving and
nothing is answering them. Check A2P, check the appointment agent is
published, then tell us.

[SHOT 14.1-01]

## Do it on your phone

Standing at the desk with a coffee. If it becomes a desk job it will not
survive a busy week.

## What you are not doing

Not reading every conversation. Not checking your numbers. Not editing
anything.

Trust the agents. If something needed you, it escalated. The daily routine is
a check, not an inspection.

## What good looks like

After a month, most mornings:

- Needs a human: empty or one
- Never contacted: empty
- Board: nothing stuck
- Total time: under five minutes

When you hit that, drop to five minutes and use the rest for coaching.

## Checklist

- [ ] Needs a human, cleared
- [ ] Today's arrivals noted and the coach told
- [ ] Yesterday's attendance marked
- [ ] Board checked for stuck cards
- [ ] Never contacted is empty
- [ ] Done on my phone, under ten minutes

## When it goes wrong

**It takes half an hour.** You are reading things you do not need to read.
Stick to the five checks.

**Needs a human is always full.** Knowledge base gaps. See 09.13 and 08.5.

**I keep forgetting.** Attach it to something you already do. Coffee, or
unlocking the gym. A habit needs a hook, not more discipline.

## Video script

**Hook.** Everything you built ran overnight. Your job is ten minutes on your
phone before the first class, and eventually five.

**Beats.**
1. On screen: phone. Run all five checks in real time, actually doing them.
   Do not talk over the whole thing, let the pace show how short it is.
2. On screen: talk to camera on check 2. The greeting-by-name point. It is the
   smallest thing here and the one members feel.
3. On screen: check 5. Say clearly: empty is normal, not empty is an alarm.
4. On screen: talk to camera. What you are not doing. Trust the agents.

**Go do.** Do the five checks tomorrow morning. Time yourself.
