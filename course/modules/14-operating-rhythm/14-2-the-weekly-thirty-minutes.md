# 14.2 The weekly thirty minutes
 
**Module:** 14 The Operating Rhythm
**Video:** ~7 min
**Needs first:** modules 09, 11
**You finish with:** a Monday routine that improves the system every week

## Why this matters

The daily routine keeps it running. The weekly routine makes it better.

Thirty minutes, same time every week. Monday morning works for most gyms,
after the daily check.

## The routine

### 1. The six numbers, five minutes

Open your Monday dashboard from 11.1. Read the six numbers from 11.2.

Compare each to last week. Write them in your tracking sheet.

**Find the one that moved most.** If it moved down, that is this week's job.

### 2. Escalations, five minutes

Open Needs a human and read `ai_escalation_reason` across the week. See 09.13.

Sort them:

- A genuine complaint, injury or discount ask: correct, nothing to fix
- A question the knowledge base should have answered: fix it now, two minutes
- **The same question for the third week running: fix it today**

### 3. Read five conversations, ten minutes

Ordinary ones that went fine. Not the escalations.

1. Does this sound like my gym?
2. Would I have said it differently, and does it matter?
3. Did it miss an obvious opportunity?

### 4. Ads, five minutes

Only if you are running them. See 10.6.

Spend, leads, cost per lead, and **joins from `source-ads`**. The last one is
the only one that matters, and it comes from your pipeline, not from Meta.

### 5. One change, five minutes

Pick one thing and do it.

Not four things. One. Then next week you will know whether it worked.

## The weekly change, and where to pick it from

| If this is weak | Do this |
|-----------------|---------|
| Booked rate | Calendar availability 03.4, then the agent 09.4 |
| Show rate | Confirmation content 09.4, reminders 03.6 |
| Join rate | Your in-person process. Not software |
| Escalations rising | Knowledge base 08.5 |
| Leads falling | Ads 10.6, or something broke |
| Nothing is weak | Improve the best agent. 11.5 question 4 |

That last row is the good problem. When nothing is broken, spend the thirty
minutes making the strongest thing stronger.

## Also weekly, in the first month only

Things that stop being necessary once you trust the system.

| Check | Until |
|-------|-------|
| Wallet usage. 01.6 | You know your normal, about a month |
| Call log for spam. 09.8 | You have confirmed it is clean |
| Every agent conversation. 09.10 | End of week 1 |

Drop each one when it stops telling you anything.

## Keep the record

One row a week: the six numbers, and the one change you made.

After three months that record tells you what actually moved the needle,
which is not usually what you expected. It is also the most useful thing you
can show us when you ask for help.

## Checklist

- [ ] Same time every week, in the calendar
- [ ] Six numbers read and recorded
- [ ] Escalations reviewed, repeats fixed
- [ ] Five ordinary conversations read
- [ ] Ad numbers checked, including joins not just leads
- [ ] One change made
- [ ] Recorded in the sheet

## When it goes wrong

**I skip it.** Put it in the calendar as an appointment with yourself, at a
time you are already at the gym and not coaching.

**Thirty minutes turns into two hours.** You are fixing things you found
rather than noting them. Note them, pick one, do that one.

**Nothing ever changes in the numbers.** Either your one change per week is
too small, or you are changing things that do not matter. Look at the weak
rate table and work the weakest one.

## Video script

**Hook.** The daily check keeps it running. This is the half hour that makes
it better, and it is the difference between a system that works and one that
compounds.

**Beats.**
1. On screen: the five parts, timed. Do the whole thing at real pace.
2. On screen: the six numbers. Find the one that moved, out loud.
3. On screen: escalations. Find a three week repeat and fix it live in the
   knowledge base.
4. On screen: talk to camera. One change. Say plainly why four changes teach
   you nothing.
5. On screen: the tracking sheet after three months. This is the argument for
   doing it at all.

**Go do.** Put thirty minutes in your calendar for Monday morning, recurring.
Then do it once.
