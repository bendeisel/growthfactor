# 11.3 Attribution, and what to trust

**Module:** 11 Reporting and Attribution
**Video:** ~8 min
**Needs first:** 11.2
**You finish with:** knowing where members come from, and knowing how much to
trust that

## Why this matters

"Where did you hear about us?" is the question every gym asks and every gym
gets a useless answer to. People genuinely do not remember.

Attribution tracks it automatically. It is better than asking, and it is
still not perfect, and knowing where it is wrong is what stops you making
bad decisions with confident numbers.

## What it does now

Attribution reporting was reworked substantially in 2026. It captures
touchpoints across paid ads, organic search, email and SMS, and the
sub-account attribution dashboard now shows revenue closed, opportunities
won, total leads, total sessions, and a revenue chart.

You can model attribution three ways.

[SHOT 11.3-01]

## The three models

Somebody sees your Instagram ad in March, searches your gym in April, clicks
a Google result, leaves, gets a follow-up email, and joins in May.

Who gets the credit?

| Model | Credit goes to | Good for |
|-------|---------------|----------|
| **First touch** | The Instagram ad | Knowing what creates awareness |
| **Last touch** | The follow-up email | Knowing what closes |
| **Linear** | Split across all four | The most balanced view |

None is correct. They answer different questions.

## Which to use for a gym

**Use first touch for deciding where to spend.**

A gym's buying cycle is long and emotional. Somebody notices you, thinks
about it for six weeks, and eventually searches your name.

Last touch will tell you that branded search converts brilliantly, so you
should spend more on branded search. That is a trap. Branded search converts
brilliantly because something else made them search for your name.

First touch tells you what started it, and what started it is what you should
buy more of.

**Use last touch for diagnosing your follow-up**, since it shows which of
your own messages actually closes people.

## Where attribution is wrong for gyms

Be honest about this, because gym buying is unusually offline.

| Reality | What attribution shows |
|---------|----------------------|
| A member told their friend at work | Direct, or organic |
| They drove past your sign for a year | Direct |
| They saw your ad, then walked in | Direct, no ad credit |
| They saw your ad on their phone, joined on a laptop | Often two separate journeys |
| Someone posted about you in a local Facebook group | Direct, or social |

**Word of mouth and walk-ins are systematically under-credited**, and for a
gym those are often your best members.

So: use attribution to compare paid channels against each other, which it
does well. Do not use it to conclude that word of mouth does not matter.

## The fix: ask as well

Attribution plus a question beats either alone.

Add one field to your intro form or your first-visit paperwork:

> `How did you hear about us?`

Free text, or a short list with "someone told me" as an option. Then compare
that to what attribution says. Where they disagree, the human answer is
usually closer for offline sources and attribution is closer for online ones.

## UTM tags, for anything you post yourself

Attribution can only see what it can identify. Anything you post manually
should carry a tag so it can be told apart.

Use trigger links or UTM parameters on:

- Links in your Instagram bio
- Links in emails you send from anywhere else
- QR codes on flyers, posters and gym signage
- Anything a partner business posts for you

A QR code on a flyer with a tagged link is the only way a flyer ever shows up
in your reporting.

## Sessions versus leads

The dashboard shows total sessions and total leads.

- **Sessions** are visits. Interesting, not important.
- **Leads** are people who gave you their details.
- **Revenue closed** is the only one that pays rent.

Do not optimise for sessions. A channel that sends a thousand sessions and
two leads is worse than one that sends fifty sessions and ten leads.

## Checklist

- [ ] I know the three models and which question each answers
- [ ] I use first touch to decide spend
- [ ] I know word of mouth is under-credited
- [ ] I also ask people how they heard about us
- [ ] Everything I post manually carries a tag
- [ ] I look at revenue closed, not sessions

## When it goes wrong

**Everything shows as Direct.** Either genuinely offline, or your links are
untagged. Tag them.

**Ads show no conversions but you know they work.** Cross-device journeys, or
people seeing the ad and walking in. Compare your ad spend period against
total new members instead, which is cruder and often truer.

**Attribution disagrees with what people tell you.** Both are partly right.
Trust people for offline, attribution for online.

## Video script

**Hook.** "How did you hear about us" is the most useless question in gym
sales, because people genuinely do not remember. Attribution does. Mostly.

**Beats.**
1. On screen: the attribution dashboard. What it shows now.
2. On screen: the three models with the March to May story. Tell the story out
   loud, it makes the models obvious.
3. On screen: talk to camera. Use first touch to decide spend. Explain the
   branded search trap properly, it is the most useful thing in the lesson.
4. On screen: the where-it-is-wrong table. Be honest. Word of mouth is
   under-credited and it is often your best source.
5. On screen: create a tagged QR link for a flyer.

**Go do.** Add "how did you hear about us" to your intro form. Then compare it
to attribution in a month.

- Exact metrics on the current attribution dashboard.
