# 05.5 POS and Tap to Pay at the desk

**Module:** 05 Payments
**Video:** ~6 min
**Needs first:** 05.1, 05.2
**You finish with:** the ability to take money at the front desk from a phone

## Why this matters

Somebody wants to buy a rash guard, pay a drop-in fee, or grab a water. If the
answer is "cash only" or "let me find the card machine", you lose the sale and
you look small.

The mobile app turns a phone into a card terminal. No extra hardware.

## What the mobile Point of Sale gives you

One module in the app covering:

- **POS**: Tap to Pay, cash, manual card entry
- **Invoices**
- **Estimates**

So the coach at the desk can take a payment, send an invoice, or quote a
private lesson from the same screen.

[SHOT 05.5-01]

## Tap to Pay, and the availability caveat

Tap to Pay lets a customer tap their card or phone against your phone. No
reader.

**Availability is limited.** Tap to Pay through the standard flow is US only,
with a beta for some other countries. Tap to Pay on Square works on supported
iPhones and is a separate route.

Check what applies to you before you promise your staff it works. If you are
outside the US, ask us rather than assuming.

## Steps

1. Install the mobile app and log in. See 00.3.

2. Open the **Payments** or POS module in the app.

   [SHOT 05.5-02]

3. Complete any device setup it asks for. On iPhone this includes granting
   the contactless permission.

4. Take a test payment of one dollar with your own card.

   [SHOT 05.5-03]

5. Refund it from **Payments > Transactions** on desktop.

## Selling at the desk

1. Open the POS module.
2. Add products, or enter a custom amount.
3. Attach it to a contact if it is a member, so the purchase lands on their
   record. Skip this for a walk-in buying a water.
4. Choose the payment method: tap, manual card, or cash.
5. Take payment.
6. Receipt by text or email.

**Attach it to the contact whenever you reasonably can.** A drop-in purchase
attached to a contact is a lead in your system. A drop-in taken as an
anonymous cash sale is a person you will never speak to again.

## Cash

Record cash sales in the POS too. It is tempting not to bother, but if half
your desk revenue is invisible, your reporting in module 11 is wrong and you
will make decisions on the wrong numbers.

## Estimates from the POS

You can build an estimate from a POS transaction and send it for the customer
to accept or reject. Useful when somebody asks about a block of private
lessons at the desk and you want to send them something rather than have the
conversation twice.

## Who should have access

Coaches taking payment need the Payments permission from 01.4, which is off
by default.

Turn it on for whoever works the desk. Leave it off for everyone else. And
check your transaction list weekly, which is basic retail discipline rather
than distrust.

## Test it

1. Take a one dollar payment with your own card via Tap to Pay.
2. Confirm it appears in Transactions on desktop within a minute.
3. Take a one dollar cash sale attached to a test contact.
4. Confirm it appears on the contact record.
5. Refund the card one.

## Checklist

- [ ] Mobile app installed on the desk phone
- [ ] Tap to Pay set up, or I know it is not available to me
- [ ] Desk staff have the Payments permission and nobody else does
- [ ] Cash sales get recorded
- [ ] Purchases attached to contacts where possible
- [ ] Tested a real payment and a refund

## When it goes wrong

**Tap to Pay not available.** Region, device, or provider. Check all three
before troubleshooting further.

**Payment taken but not showing.** Give it a minute, then check the app is
logged into the right sub-account.

**Coach cannot see the POS.** Payments permission is off. See 01.4.

**Cash sales missing from reporting.** Nobody is recording them. This is a
habit problem, and the fix is making it part of closing up.

## Video script

**Hook.** Somebody wants to buy a rash guard. If your answer is "cash only",
you just lost the sale and looked small doing it.

**Beats.**
1. On screen: phone. Open the POS module. Show the three things in one place.
2. On screen: take a real Tap to Pay payment on camera. Card against phone.
   This shot sells the lesson on its own.
3. On screen: talk to camera. The availability caveat. Be straight about it.
4. On screen: attaching a sale to a contact. Say the line about an anonymous
   cash sale being a person you never speak to again.
5. On screen: desktop, the transaction appearing.

**Go do.** Take a one dollar payment from your own card on your own phone.
Then refund it.

## Verify on screen

- Current Tap to Pay availability by country and provider.
- Whether the app module is called Payments, POS, or Point of Sale.
- Whether cash is a recordable payment method in the current release.
