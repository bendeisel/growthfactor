# 03.1 Calendar types, and which a gym uses

**Module:** 03 Calendars
**Video:** ~6 min
**Needs first:** module 01
**You finish with:** knowing which calendar type to reach for, and why the
wrong one causes months of pain

## Why this matters

Pick the wrong calendar type and you will fight it forever. Changing type
later usually means rebuilding, and rebuilding means every link you have
shared stops working.

Five minutes of choosing correctly saves that.

## The types

[SHOT 03.1-01]

| Type | What it does | Gym use |
|------|-------------|---------|
| **Personal / Simple** | One person's availability, one booking per slot | A specific coach's personal training slots |
| **Round Robin** | Distributes bookings across several people | Intro sessions where any coach can take it. **This is your intro calendar** |
| **Collective** | Requires several people free at once | Rare in a gym. Skip it |
| **Class** | Many people book the same slot, up to a capacity | Group intro sessions, open mats, seminars |
| **Service** | A menu of services, grouped, on one link | Multi-service gyms. Massage, PT, assessments, on one page |

## What a typical gym needs

Three calendars. Not fifteen.

1. **Intro / Trial**, round robin. The one the appointment agent books into.
2. **Class Intro**, class type, if you run group intros rather than one to one.
3. **Personal Training**, service or personal, if you sell PT.

That is it. Resist building a calendar per coach, per program, per time of
day. Every extra calendar is another thing the agent has to choose between and
another thing that can be misconfigured.

## Why round robin for the intro calendar

Three reasons, and they all matter to the agent.

1. **Coverage.** If one coach is off, the slot is still bookable. A personal
   calendar goes empty when its owner is away, and the agent then tells your
   hottest lead there is nothing available.
2. **Fair distribution.** Bookings spread across the team.
3. **More availability shown.** The agent offers two specific times, per 09.4.
   Round robin means it almost always has two to offer.

The one downside: the lead does not choose their coach. For an intro session,
almost nobody cares.

## Service calendars, when they earn their place

A service calendar gives you a single link showing a menu of what you offer,
grouped into categories. A member picks the service, then the time.

Worth it if you sell several distinct bookable things: assessments, PT,
massage, nutrition consults, private lessons. Not worth it if you have one
intro offer.

The categories are what make it good. A martial arts gym can group `Private
Lessons` and `Assessments` separately, and the member sees a clean menu rather
than a list of fifteen options.

## Naming

Name calendars for what they are, from the member's point of view, because
the name shows up on the booking page.

Good: `Free Intro Session`, `Kids Trial Class`, `Personal Training`.
Bad: `Cal 1`, `RR Intro v2`, `Main`.

## Checklist

- [ ] I know which type my intro calendar is, and it is round robin
- [ ] I have three calendars or fewer
- [ ] Names read well to a member, not to me

## Video script

**Hook.** Pick the wrong calendar type now and you rebuild it in three months,
and every link you have ever shared breaks.

**Beats.**
1. On screen: the type selector. Five types, one line each, fast.
2. On screen: talk to camera. A gym needs three calendars. Say the number,
   people build fifteen.
3. On screen: a round robin with coaches on it. The coverage argument. Say the
   thing about a lead being told nothing is available.
4. On screen: a service calendar member view. Ten seconds, only for gyms who
   need it.

**Go do.** Open Calendars and check what type your intro calendar is. If it is
not round robin, message us before changing it.
