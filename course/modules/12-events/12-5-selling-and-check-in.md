# 12.5 Selling tickets and check-in

**Module:** 12 Events (Labs beta)
**Video:** ~7 min
**Needs first:** 12.4
**You finish with:** tickets sold and a check-in process that works on a busy
morning

## Why this matters

Two jobs, and the second one is where events go wrong.

Selling is straightforward. Check-in happens with forty people arriving in ten
minutes, one person on the desk, and a coach who needs to start.

## Selling

### Where to send people

One link, everywhere. Your event page.

| Channel | How |
|---------|-----|
| In class | Tell them, then text the link the same day |
| Your list | Email and SMS to the relevant segment |
| Social | Post the link, get coaches to share |
| Lapsed members | A direct invitation. See 12.3 |
| Paid ads | Acquisition events only |

### Segment who you send it to

Do not blast everyone. A comp day invitation to a member who has trained twice
is noise.

| Event | Send to |
|-------|---------|
| Guest seminar | Active members, plus your local martial arts contacts |
| Belt test | Those eligible, and their families |
| Comp day | Active members, all levels, with reassurance for beginners |
| Open house | Leads, lapsed members, and members to bring friends |
| Kids camp | Parents on your list, plus local paid ads |

[SHOT 12.5-01]

### The reminder that fills events

Most tickets sell in two windows: the week you announce it, and the three days
before.

The middle is quiet, and gym owners panic in the middle and discount. Do not.

Plan three sends: announce, midpoint nudge, and a final call three days out.
The final call sells more than the midpoint nudge, every time.

## Check-in on the day

### Before the day

1. Export the attendee list to a spreadsheet the day before. Beta insurance,
   from 12.1. Print it if you like paper.
2. Check the list for anyone you should greet personally: first-timers,
   non-members, somebody who came back after lapsing.
3. Brief whoever is on the desk.

### On the day

1. Open the event on the mobile app or a tablet.
2. Check people in as they arrive.

   [SHOT 12.5-02]

3. Anyone not on the list, take payment on the spot with the POS from 05.5,
   then check them in.

### Make it fast

Forty people in ten minutes means about fifteen seconds each. So:

- One person on check-in, doing nothing else
- Have the list open before anyone arrives, not loading as they queue
- Search by first name, it is faster
- Do not solve problems in the queue. Wave them through, note it, sort it
  after

That last rule is the one. A single ticket problem worked through in the
queue stops the whole line and makes the event feel disorganised before it
starts.

### Walk-ins

Always some. Have a plan.

- Decide in advance whether you will take them and at what price
- Take payment on the phone with Tap to Pay, per 05.5
- **Get their details.** A walk-in you take cash from and do not record is a
  lead you will never speak to again

[SHOT 12.5-03]

## After check-in, before you forget

While it is fresh, mark the no-shows. It takes two minutes and it powers the
follow-up in 12.6.

A no-show at an event they paid for is somebody with a reason, and it is worth
a message.

## Checklist

- [ ] Announce, midpoint, final call, all planned
- [ ] Sent to a segment, not everyone
- [ ] Attendee list exported the day before
- [ ] People to greet personally identified
- [ ] One person on check-in doing nothing else
- [ ] Walk-in plan and price decided
- [ ] Walk-in details captured, not just cash taken
- [ ] No-shows marked the same day

## When it goes wrong

**Check-in is slow and a queue forms.** Stop solving problems in the queue.
Wave people through and fix afterwards.

**Somebody paid but is not on the list.** Take their word, check them in, sort
it after. Never argue with a paying customer at your own event.

**The app will not load on the day.** Your exported spreadsheet. This is
exactly why it exists.

**Walk-ins outnumbered ticket holders.** Your promotion reached people late.
Move your final call earlier next time.

## Video script

**Hook.** Selling the tickets is the easy half. The hard half is forty people
arriving in ten minutes with one person on the desk.

**Beats.**
1. On screen: the send plan. Announce, midpoint, final call. Say the final
   call sells more, and that the quiet middle is not a reason to discount.
2. On screen: segmented sending.
3. On screen: export the list the day before. Say why, hold up a printed one.
4. On screen: check-in on a phone, several people in a row, fast. Say the
   fifteen seconds each figure.
5. On screen: talk to camera. Do not solve problems in the queue. Say it
   twice, it is the rule that saves the morning.
6. On screen: a walk-in, Tap to Pay, and capturing their details.

**Go do.** Before your next event, export the attendee list the day before and
decide your walk-in price.

## Verify on screen

- Whether check-in is available on the mobile app and how it behaves offline.
- Whether attendee export is available in the beta.
- Whether a walk-in can be added and paid for from the check-in screen.
