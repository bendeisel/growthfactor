# 12.2 Ticketed, RSVP, and which to use

**Module:** 12 Events (Labs beta)
**Video:** ~6 min
**Needs first:** 12.1
**You finish with:** the right event type for each thing your gym runs

## Why this matters

You can create free, paid, RSVP, in-person, online and hybrid events. Picking
wrong means either charging for something that should be free, or getting
sixty RSVPs and twelve people.

Events is in Labs, so the options here can change.

## The two dimensions

### Ticketed or RSVP

| | Ticketed | RSVP |
|---|---------|------|
| What it is | A ticket, usually paid, sometimes free with capacity | A registration, no payment |
| Enforces capacity | Yes | Yes |
| Money | Yes | No |
| Show rate | High. They paid | Lower |
| Use for | Seminars, workshops, comp entry, paid socials | Open houses, member events, free intro days |

### In person, online, hybrid

Mostly obvious for a gym. Hybrid is worth remembering for a seminar you also
want to stream to members who cannot attend.

## The show rate problem, and the fix

An RSVP event will get more registrations and fewer attendees. Consistently.
People say yes to free things and then it rains.

**The fix: charge a small amount, even for something you intend to be free.**

Ten dollars, refunded on the day or credited against a membership. Show rate
goes up substantially, because people turn up for things they paid for.

For a free seminar with a guest instructor, ten dollars filters out the
people who were never coming and costs you nothing in goodwill.

## What each gym event should be

| Event | Type | Why |
|-------|------|-----|
| Guest instructor seminar | Ticketed, paid | Real value, real cost, high commitment |
| Belt test or grading | Ticketed, paid if you charge a grading fee | Fee collection and attendance in one |
| Comp day or in-house tournament | Ticketed, paid | Entry fee, brackets need known numbers |
| Open house | RSVP, free | Low commitment on purpose, you want footfall |
| Bring a friend day | RSVP, free | Members bring guests, no barrier |
| Kids camp | Ticketed, paid | It is a product |
| Members social | RSVP, free | |
| Charity or fundraiser | Ticketed, paid | The payment is the point |

## Capacity

Set it honestly, per 03.3. A seminar that says 40 and fits 25 comfortably
produces a bad seminar and a refund conversation.

Sold out is also a marketing asset. An event that sells out is easier to sell
next time.

[SHOT 12.2-02]

## Pricing a gym event

| Event | Typical |
|-------|---------|
| Guest instructor seminar, 2 to 3 hours | $40 to $90 |
| In-house workshop by your own coach | $20 to $40, or free for members |
| Kids camp, a day | $40 to $70 |
| Comp entry | $30 to $60 |
| Open house | Free |

**Member versus non-member pricing.** Two tiers, with members cheaper or
free, does two things: it rewards membership visibly, and it gives non-members
a reason to ask about joining. Set that up if the platform supports tiers, and
if it does not yet, run two ticket types.

## Video script

**Hook.** Free events get twice the registrations and half the people. Here is
the ten dollar fix.

**Beats.**
1. On screen: the type selection. Ticketed versus RSVP in thirty seconds.
2. On screen: talk to camera. The show rate problem. Then the ten dollar fix,

3. On screen: the gym event table. Fast.
4. On screen: capacity, set honestly. Mention sold out as a marketing asset.
5. On screen: two ticket types, member and non-member. Say why the gap is a
   sales tool.
