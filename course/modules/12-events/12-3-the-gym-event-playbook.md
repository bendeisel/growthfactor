# 12.3 The gym event playbook

**Module:** 12 Events (Labs beta)
**Video:** ~9 min
**Needs first:** 12.2
**You finish with:** a year of events planned, and knowing what each one is for

## Why this matters

Most gyms run events by accident. Somebody suggests a seminar, it happens,
sixteen people come, and nobody knows whether it was worth it.

Events do three distinct jobs. Know which job each one is doing before you
plan it, and you stop running the wrong event for the outcome you wanted.

## The three jobs

| Job | What it does | Measured by |
|-----|-------------|-------------|
| **Acquisition** | Gets non-members through the door | New members within 30 days |
| **Retention** | Gives members a reason to stay | Attendance, and lapse rate afterwards |
| **Revenue** | Makes money on the day | Ticket revenue minus costs |

Most events do one well and one badly. A guest seminar makes money and
retains members, and acquires almost nobody, because the people who come are
already training somewhere.

**Trying to make one event do all three is why events feel disappointing.**

## The events, and what each is actually for

### Open house

**Job: acquisition.** The only one that is genuinely built for it.

Free, RSVP, a weekend morning. Classes running, coaches available, no
pressure. People wander in with their kids.

Get it right: run actual classes so there is something to watch, have a
sign-up offer ready on the day, and make sure your appointment agent picks up
everyone who registered but did not come. See 12.6.

### Guest instructor seminar

**Job: revenue and retention.** Not acquisition, whatever you hope.

The people who pay $70 to learn from a visiting black belt already train.
Often at other gyms, which is fine, they are seeing your facility.

Worth running for the members. Do not judge it on new members.

### Belt test or grading

**Job: retention**, and it is the strongest retention event a martial arts
gym has.

Families come, photos get taken, everybody is at their most positive.

**Fire the review agent that evening.** See 09.7. This single habit will
out-review every other gym in your town within a year.

### In-house comp or comp day

**Job: retention**, strongly. Members who compete stay far longer than members
who do not.

Low barrier, in-house, friendly. It is the on-ramp to real competition for
people who would never enter an open tournament.

### Bring a friend day

**Job: acquisition**, and the cheapest acquisition you have.

A friend brought by a member converts better than any ad, because they arrive
with a trusted person.

Free, RSVP, and make sure every guest gets captured as a contact rather than
just walking in and out. That is the whole reason to run it through Events
rather than a group chat.

### Kids camp

**Job: revenue and acquisition.** School holidays, working parents, a real
need.

Often your best acquisition event, because parents who use the camp meet you,
and their kids ask to come back.

### Workshop by your own coach

**Job: retention and revenue.** Cheap to run, no travel costs, and it shows
off your own coaches, which is better for your gym than showing off somebody
else's.

## A year of events

Not every gym needs all of these. Four to six a year is plenty.

| Month | Event | Job |
|-------|-------|-----|
| January | Open house | Acquisition |
| March | Guest seminar | Revenue, retention |
| April | Belt test | Retention |
| June | Bring a friend day | Acquisition |
| July | Kids camp | Revenue, acquisition |
| September | Open house | Acquisition |
| October | In-house comp | Retention |
| December | Members social, and belt test | Retention |

Two acquisition events a year, in January and September, which are the two
months people actually start things. See 10.5.

## Promoting an event

In order of what works for a gym:

1. **Your own members.** Tell them in class, in person. Most effective by a
   distance.
2. **Your list.** Email and text everyone relevant, including lapsed members.
   An event is the best possible reason to contact a lapsed member, because
   it is an invitation rather than a pitch.
3. **Organic social.** Post it, and get your coaches to post it.
4. **Paid ads.** Only for acquisition events, and only if it is big enough to
   justify the spend.

**Point 2 is underused.** A lapsed member who ignores three win-back texts
will come to a comp day. Invite them. See 09.6.

to fill. Better to know before you book the flights.

## Checklist

- [ ] I know which job each event I run is doing
- [ ] I am not judging a seminar on new members
- [ ] Four to six events planned for the year
- [ ] Two acquisition events, in January and September
- [ ] Lapsed members get invited to events
- [ ] Every event is costed before committing
- [ ] Review agent fires after gradings

## Video script

**Hook.** Most gyms run a seminar, sixteen people come, and nobody knows
whether it was worth doing. That is because nobody decided what it was for.

**Beats.**
1. On screen: the three jobs. Acquisition, retention, revenue.
2. On screen: talk to camera. The guest seminar truth. It will not get you
   members and that is fine, stop judging it that way.
3. On screen: each event and its job. Steady pace.
4. On screen: the belt test point. Say the review agent thing clearly, it is
   the best tip in the module.
5. On screen: the year plan. January and September, and why.
6. On screen: talk to camera. Invite your lapsed members. A comp day
   invitation works where three win-back texts did not.
7. On screen: the costing table, filled in for a real seminar.

**Go do.** Plan four events for the next twelve months, and write down what
job each one is doing.
