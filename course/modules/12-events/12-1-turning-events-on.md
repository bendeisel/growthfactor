# 12.1 Turning Events on in Labs

**Module:** 12 Events (Labs beta)
**Video:** ~5 min
**Needs first:** module 05
**You finish with:** Events visible in your sub-account

## Why this matters

Events brings the whole event lifecycle inside the platform: build the page,
sell the tickets, take registrations, manage attendees, check people in, and
automate what happens before and after.

For a gym that means seminars, belt tests, comp days, open houses and
workshops stop being run on a mix of Eventbrite, a spreadsheet and a group
chat.

**Events is in Labs.** It is a beta. It works, and it can change. See 12.6 for
what to do if it does.

## How access works

Labs access is controlled at agency level, which is us.

An agency can enable Events for all sub-accounts or selected ones, control
whether it appears in Labs for a given sub-account, and roll it out
gradually. No requesting access from HighLevel, and no waiting.

So if you cannot see Events, ask us and we will switch it on.

[SHOT 12.1-01]

## Steps

1. Ask us to enable Events for your sub-account. One message.

2. Open **Settings > Labs**.

   [SHOT 12.1-02]

3. Find Events and turn it on.

4. Events appears in your navigation.

   [SHOT 12.1-03]

## What you need working first

Events sells tickets, so payments must be working.

- Stripe connected. See 05.1
- At least one product, if you are selling paid tickets. See 05.2
- A2P approved, if you want event reminders by text. See 01.2

## What beta means, practically

Four things to expect.

1. **Features can change** between now and general release. A setting may move
   or gain options.
2. **Some edges are rough.** Occasional things that feel unfinished.
3. **It is real, not a toy.** People take real money through it.
4. **Keep a fallback.** For your first event, export your attendee list to a
   spreadsheet the day before. If anything goes odd on the day, you have a
   list and a pen.

That fourth point costs you two minutes and it removes all the risk from
trying a beta on an event that matters.

## Should you use it, or Eventbrite

Honest comparison.

| | Events | Eventbrite |
|---|--------|-----------|
| Attendees land in your CRM | Yes, automatically | Export and import |
| Automated follow-up | Yes, your workflows | No |
| Your branding | Yes | Theirs |
| Ticket fees | Your payment processor only | Their fee on top |
| Discovery, people finding it | No | Some |
| Maturity | Beta | Mature |

The deciding factor for a gym: **an attendee who lands in your CRM can be
followed up by an agent.** An attendee in an Eventbrite export is a
spreadsheet nobody opens.

You are running a seminar to get members, not to run a seminar.
