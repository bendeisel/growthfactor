# 09.11 Publishing, channels and the human takeover rule

**Module:** 09 Your Agents
**Video:** ~7 min
**Needs first:** 09.10
**You finish with:** your agents live, on the right channels, and stepping
back when you step in

## Why this matters

Publishing is the moment your agents start talking to real people. Two
minutes of care here, and one rule about human takeover, is the difference
between a system you trust and one you turn off after a bad week.

## Before you publish anything

| Gate | Applies to |
|------|-----------|
| Knowledge base complete, no `[` remaining | All agents. Hard gate for 1 and 6 |
| A2P approved | Anything that texts |
| Calendar configured with real availability | Appointment, navigator, voice |
| Twelve message test passed, six safety ones especially | All agents |
| `ZZ Blocked` test passed | All agents |
| Call routing tested by phone | Voice agent only |

## Publishing

1. Open the agent.
2. Confirm you are looking at the version you tested.
3. Publish.

   [SHOT 09.11-01]

4. Confirm the status changed.
5. Send it a real message from your own phone.

Step 5 every time. A published agent that does not answer is worse than a
paused one, because you think it is working.

## The order to go live in

Do not publish all six on the same day.

| Day | Publish | Then |
|-----|---------|------|
| 1 | Appointment | Submit forms, watch it for a day |
| 2 | Follow-up | Tag a test contact, read the sequence |
| 3 | Review | Fire it at yourself |
| 5 | Navigator | Read every conversation for a week |
| 8 | Voice | Call it repeatedly, check the log daily for a week |
| 10 | Reactivation | Small batch first, see below |

Staggering means when something looks wrong you know which agent caused it.

### Reactivation goes last, and small

It talks to your existing members and ex-members, which is the highest risk
audience you have.

1. Set the sweep cap low. Ten a day. See 06.3.
2. Run it for three days.
3. Read every conversation.
4. Then raise the cap gradually.

Do not point it at 800 lost leads on day one.

## The human takeover rule

The most important behaviour in the system, and the one members notice.

**When you reply manually in Conversations, the agents stop.**

1. You type a reply and send it
2. `staff-handling` is applied
3. Every agent steps back from that contact
4. You own the conversation

That is what makes the whole thing safe to run. The moment you see something
going wrong, you type, and the agent is out.

[SHOT 09.11-03]

### Giving it back

Remove `staff-handling` and the agents resume.

**People forget this constantly.** A contact left with `staff-handling` never
hears from an agent again. It is the single most common cause of a member
quietly going dark in the system.

Two habits that fix it:

- Remove it when you close the conversation, as part of finishing.
- Check for stale ones in the quarterly clean. See 02.4.

### When to take over

- Anything an agent escalated
- Anything that feels off when you read it
- Anybody upset
- Anybody you know personally and want to talk to yourself

You do not need a reason. Take over whenever you want.

### When not to

Do not take over just because you would have phrased it differently. If the
agent handled it fine, leave it. Owners who take over every conversation end
up doing all the work they bought the system to avoid.

## Pausing

Any agent, any time, one toggle. Nothing is lost.

Pause when: something is wrong, you are changing something significant, or
you are away and would rather nothing ran.

An agent paused for a week costs you nothing except the leads it would have
answered.

- [ ] I know a manual reply stops the agents

- [ ] I know how to pause and roll back

## When it goes wrong

**Published but not answering.** Channel enabled? Contact carrying a blocking
tag? A2P approved? In that order.

**An agent replied while I was mid conversation.** `staff-handling` was not
applied. Check with us, that is wiring.

**A member went silent in the system.** Stale `staff-handling`. Check that
first, always.

**Something went out that should not have.** Pause the agent, read what
happened, then fix. Pause first, diagnose second.
