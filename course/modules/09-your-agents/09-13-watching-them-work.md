# 09.13 Watching them work

**Module:** 09 Your Agents
**Video:** ~8 min
**Needs first:** 09.11
**You finish with:** a weekly habit that makes your agents better every month

## Why this matters

Your agents will not improve on their own. They will do exactly what they did
last week, forever.

They improve when you read what they said, notice what they could not handle,
and fix that in the knowledge base. Twenty minutes a week, and the difference
compounds.

Most people skip this and then wonder why their agents feel stuck at
"reasonably good".

## Week 1: read everything

Every conversation an agent had. All of them.

It is fewer than you think and it is the fastest learning available. You will
find things you would never have predicted: a phrase that reads oddly, a
question you did not anticipate, a program somebody assumed you offer.

Fix each one as you find it. Most fixes are knowledge base edits that take
two minutes.

## The weekly review, twenty minutes

From week 2 onwards. Put it in your calendar.

### 1. Escalations, five minutes

Open the **Needs a human** smart list from 02.2 and read
`ai_escalation_reason` across the last week.

**This is the single most valuable list in your account.** It is a running
record of everything your agents cannot do, written by the situation itself.

Sort what you find:

| Escalation type | Action |
|-----------------|--------|
| A question the KB does not answer | Write the answer in. Two minutes |
| A complaint | Correct escalation. Nothing to fix |
| An injury or medical question | Correct. Nothing to fix |
| A discount request | Correct. Nothing to fix |
| The same question three weeks running | Knowledge base gap. Fix it today |

**The repeats are the gold.** The same question escalating three times means
three leads got a worse experience than they needed to, and one FAQ entry
fixes it permanently.

[SHOT 09.13-02]

### 2. Read five conversations, ten minutes

Not the escalations. Five ordinary ones that went fine.

Ask three questions:

1. Does this sound like my gym?
2. Would I have said it differently, and does that matter?
3. Did it miss an obvious opportunity?

Number 3 is where the improvements come from. An agent that answered the
question but did not offer a trial is doing half the job.

### 3. Check the numbers, five minutes

From module 11:

| Number | What it tells you |
|--------|------------------|
| Conversations started | Volume |
| Bookings made by agents | Whether they convert |
| Escalation rate | Whether the KB is thin |
| Response time | Whether they are actually fast |

**Escalation rate is the health metric.** Rising means your knowledge base is
falling behind. Falling toward zero can mean the agents are answering things
they should escalate, which is worse. You want it steady and low, not zero.

## What good looks like after three months

- Escalation rate steady and low
- Almost every escalation is a genuine complaint, injury or discount ask,
  not a knowledge gap
- You read five conversations and would not change any of them
- Agent bookings are a meaningful share of total bookings
- You have stopped checking daily because you trust it

## The three signals something is wrong

### Escalations climbing

Knowledge base is behind. Something changed at the gym and nobody updated it.
See 08.5.

### Conversations getting longer

Agents taking six messages to do what took three. Usually a prompt edit that
made them chattier. Costs more and converts worse. See 07.5.

### Bookings falling with steady leads

In order: check calendar availability, check the agent is published, read five
conversations and find where they stall.

Availability is the answer more often than anything else. See 03.4.

## Keep a list of what you fixed

Simple, in a note.

| Date | What happened | What I changed |
|------|--------------|----------------|

After three months this is a record of your gym's actual FAQ, written by real
enquiries. It is also what you hand a new staff member.

## When to bring us in

- Escalation rate climbing and you cannot see why
- An agent behaving differently after no change from you
- Anything involving the shared rules block
- Something you have tried twice
- You want a seventh agent

## When it goes wrong

**I do not have time for twenty minutes a week.** Do the five minute
escalation review only. It is most of the value.

**Everything looks fine and I am not finding anything.** Good. Drop to
fortnightly. Do not stop entirely, because the day something changes at the
gym is the day the knowledge base goes stale.

**The same escalation keeps appearing after I fixed it.** The knowledge base
edit did not take, or a crawled page contradicts it. See 08.5.

## Video script

**Hook.** Your agents will never get better on their own. They will do exactly
what they did last week, forever. Twenty minutes a week changes that, and
almost nobody does it.

**Beats.**
1. On screen: week 1, reading everything. Show a real conversation, find
   something worth fixing, fix it in the knowledge base on camera. Two
   minutes, start to finish.
2. On screen: the Needs a human list. Read three escalation reasons out loud.
   Spot a repeat. Say: three leads got a worse experience and one FAQ entry
   fixes it forever.
3. On screen: read five ordinary conversations. Ask question 3 out loud, did
   it miss an opportunity, and find one that did.
4. On screen: escalation rate. Explain why zero is not the goal.
5. On screen: talk to camera. What good looks like at three months.

**Go do.** Book twenty minutes in your calendar, weekly, starting next
Monday. Then read your escalation list right now.
