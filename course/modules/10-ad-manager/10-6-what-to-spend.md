# 10.6 What to spend, and when to kill a campaign

**Module:** 10 Ad Manager
**Video:** ~8 min
**Needs first:** 10.4, module 04
**You finish with:** a budget, a target cost per member, and a rule for
stopping

## Why this matters

Two failure modes. Spending too little to learn anything, and spending too
long on something that was never going to work.

Both are fixed with numbers you can work out in ten minutes.

## Work out what a member is worth

Before deciding what to spend, know what you are buying.

| Line | Your gym |
|------|----------|
| Monthly membership | `$149` |
| Average months a member stays | `9` |
| **Lifetime value** | **`$1,341`** |

**Be honest about the months.** Owners guess 24 and the real number is
often 7 to 12. If you do not know, look at when your current members joined
and how many of last year's intake are still here.

That lifetime value is the number that makes everything else decidable.

## The chain of numbers

Work down. Each one is a rate you can measure once you have run for a month.

| Step | Typical for a gym | Yours |
|------|------------------|-------|
| Cost per lead | $8 to $25 | |
| Leads to booked | 40 to 60% | |
| Booked to showed | 50 to 70% | |
| Showed to joined | 40 to 60% | |
| **Cost per member** | **$60 to $200** | |

Worked through: $15 per lead, 50% book, 60% show, 50% join. That is
1 member per 6.7 leads, so about $100 per member.

Against a $1,341 lifetime value, $100 is a good trade. If it comes out at
$600, it is not.

## What to spend

**Month 1, learning.** $20 to $30 a day. Enough to generate fifty leads in a
month, which is the minimum to learn anything.

Spending $5 a day produces four leads a month and tells you nothing except
that you wasted two months finding out.

**Month 2 onwards.** Once you know your cost per member and it is well below
lifetime value, spend as much as you can handle.

**Handle** is the real limit, not budget. If you can run six intros a day and
you are getting eight leads a day, you are already at capacity. More spend
buys leads that do not get worked properly, which makes everything worse.

## Where the money actually goes wrong

Almost never the ad. Look at the chain.

| Symptom | Look at |
|---------|---------|
| Cost per lead high | Radius, offer, creative. 10.2 |
| Leads not booking | Agent response, calendar availability. 09.4, 03.4 |
| Booking but not showing | Confirmation and reminders. 09.4, 03.6 |
| Showing but not joining | Your in-person sales process. Not software |

**That last row is where most gyms actually lose.** They blame the ads. The
ads delivered somebody into the building. What happened after that is on the
gym.

If leads are booking and showing and not joining, more ad spend makes it
worse, not better.

## When to kill a campaign

Rules, in order.

### Kill it if, after fifty leads

- Cost per member is above a third of lifetime value, and you have already
  fixed everything downstream
- Leads are consistently outside your area or clearly junk
- Cost per lead is three times the top of the typical range with no
  explanation

### Do not kill it if

- It has run less than a week
- Fewer than fifty leads
- It is December
- The problem is downstream. Fix that first
- You changed something three days ago

### Before killing, change the offer

The offer is the biggest lever. A campaign failing on a free trial can work on
a paid challenge, or the reverse.

Duplicate, change the offer, run both. See 10.5. Kill it only after two
offers have failed.

## What to check weekly

Five minutes, alongside the module 11 review.

1. Spend
2. Leads
3. Cost per lead
4. Bookings from `source-ads`
5. Joins from `source-ads`

Numbers 4 and 5 come from your pipeline, not from Meta. Meta will tell you
about leads. Only your CRM tells you about members, and members are the point.

This is exactly why module 04 and the `source-ads` tag exist.

## Checklist

- [ ] I know my lifetime value, honestly
- [ ] I know my target cost per member
- [ ] I am spending enough to get fifty leads in a month
- [ ] I check joins from `source-ads`, not just leads
- [ ] I fix downstream before increasing spend
- [ ] I have a kill rule and I follow it
- [ ] I try a second offer before killing

## When it goes wrong

**Cost per member looks terrible.** Work up the chain and find which step is
weak. It is rarely the ad.

**Great leads, no members.** Your in-person process. The ads are working.

**Cannot tell what ads produced.** `source-ads` is not applying, or your
pipeline is not maintained. See 06.3 and module 04.

**January was brilliant, now it is awful.** Seasonality. See 10.5.

## Video script

**Hook.** Before you spend a dollar, work out what a member is worth to you.
Most gym owners guess twenty four months. The real number is usually under
twelve, and it changes every decision after this.

**Beats.**
1. On screen: the lifetime value calculation. Do it on camera with real
   numbers. Push on the honesty of the months figure.
2. On screen: the chain of numbers. Work the example through to cost per
   member.
3. On screen: talk to camera. What to spend. Say plainly that $5 a day
   teaches you nothing.
4. On screen: the where-it-goes-wrong table. Dwell on the last row. Say
   clearly: if they show up and do not join, that is not the ads.
5. On screen: the kill rules, and change the offer before killing.
6. On screen: pipeline filtered by `source-ads` showing actual joins. That
   number, not Meta's.

**Go do.** Work out your lifetime value today. Honestly. Then work out what
you can afford per member.
