# 10.5 Pausing, editing, duplicating

**Module:** 10 Ad Manager
**Video:** ~5 min
**Needs first:** 10.3
**You finish with:** knowing when to touch a campaign and when to leave it
alone

## Why this matters

The most common way gym owners waste ad money is editing a campaign every day.

Every significant edit restarts learning. The platform needs a run of
conversions before it knows who to show your ad to. Edit it on day two and
you throw that away and pay for the learning again.

## The rule

**Leave a new campaign alone for seven days or fifty leads, whichever comes
first.**

It will look bad on day one. Cost per lead will be high, delivery will be
uneven. That is normal and it is not information.

## What restarts learning, and what does not

| Change | Restarts learning? |
|--------|-------------------|
| Budget change over about 20% | Yes |
| Audience change | Yes |
| Creative change | Yes |
| Objective change | Yes, completely |
| Small budget change under 20% | Usually not |
| Pausing and unpausing within a day | Usually not |
| Editing the ad copy text | Often yes |
| Turning off one ad in a set of several | No |

The pattern: anything that changes who sees it or what they see resets it.

## Pausing

Pause when:

- You are full and cannot take more intros
- The offer has ended
- Something is broken downstream, an agent is off or the calendar is empty
- You are away and cannot handle leads

**Pause rather than delete.** A paused campaign keeps its history and its
learning. Deleted is gone, along with the audience data.

Unpausing after a long pause behaves like a new campaign. Expect a learning
period again.

## Editing

When you genuinely need to change something:

1. Wait until the learning period is over.
2. Change **one** thing.
3. Wait another week.
4. Compare.

Changing three things at once means you learn nothing about any of them.

## Duplicating

The right way to test something new.

Instead of editing a working campaign, duplicate it, change one thing in the
copy, and run both.

1. Find the campaign, duplicate it.
2. Rename to the format from 10.3, with what changed.

   > `Meta | Lead form | 6wk challenge | Mar26`

3. Change the one thing.
4. Run both.
5. After a week or fifty leads, keep the winner, pause the loser.

Your original keeps running and keeps working while you test.

[SHOT 10.5-03]

## What is worth testing

Ranked by impact:

1. **The offer.** Biggest lever by far. Free trial versus paid challenge.
2. **The creative.** Different class footage, different coach.
3. **The hook**, the first line of copy.
4. Audience. Only after the first three.

Do not test button colours or emoji. Test the offer.

## Seasonality for a gym

Worth planning around.

| Period | What happens |
|--------|-------------|
| January | Highest volume, highest cost, lowest quality. Real but soft |
| Feb to April | Best months. Serious people, reasonable costs |
| Summer | Slower for adults, good for kids camps |
| September | Second January. Routines restart, very good |
| December | Worst month. Most gyms should pause and save the budget |

Do not judge a campaign that ran only in December. And do not conclude ads
work brilliantly based on January alone.

## Checklist

- [ ] I leave new campaigns alone for a week or fifty leads
- [ ] I change one thing at a time
- [ ] I duplicate to test rather than editing the working one
- [ ] I pause rather than delete
- [ ] I test the offer before anything else
- [ ] I know December and January distort everything

## When it goes wrong

**Performance dropped after an edit.** Learning restarted. Wait it out, or
revert and let the original recover.

**Costs climbed with no changes.** Audience fatigue. The same people have seen
it too often. Refresh the creative.

**A duplicate performs worse than the original with identical settings.**
Normal variance, and the new one has no learning. Give it the full week.

**Hook.** The most expensive habit in gym advertising is opening the campaign

**Beats.**
