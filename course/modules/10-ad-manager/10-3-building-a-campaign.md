# 10.3 Building a campaign

**Module:** 10 Ad Manager
**Video:** ~10 min
**Needs first:** 10.2
**You finish with:** a live campaign sending leads into your CRM

## Why this matters

This is the doing lesson. Follow it with your account open.

## Steps

1. Open **Marketing > Ad Manager** and create a campaign.

   [SHOT 10.3-01]

2. **Objective:** Leads.

3. **Name it properly.** You will have eight of these in a year.

   Format: `Platform | Campaign type | Offer | Month`

   > `Meta | Lead form | Free week | Mar26`

4. **Audience.** Location with your radius from 10.2, age range, and your
   member list as an exclusion.

   [SHOT 10.3-02]

5. **Budget.** Daily, not lifetime, while learning. See 10.6.

6. **Schedule.** Run continuously. Do not day-part until you have data.

7. **Creative.** Your real class footage. Upload video and images.

8. **Copy.** See below.

9. **The lead form.** See below. This is the part that matters most.

10. Review and publish.

## Writing the ad copy

Short. Written for somebody scrolling on a phone who was not looking for a
gym.

**The structure that works:**

Line 1, the person and the problem.
Line 2, what you are offering.
Line 3, the ask.

> `Never trained BJJ and slightly terrified of walking in? That's most of our
> Tuesday beginners class.`
>
> `Free week trial, no gear needed, we lend you everything.`
>
> `Tap below and we'll text you two times that work.`

What that does: names the fear, normalises it, removes the friction, and sets
the expectation that they will get a text. That last line raises answer rates
when the agent texts, because they were told it was coming.

**What not to write:**

- "Are you ready to transform your life?"
- Anything with three or more emoji in a row
- A list of your facilities
- Anything about being the best in town

## The lead form

Where most gyms lose the value of the campaign.

[SHOT 10.3-03]

### Fields

Ask for four:

1. First name
2. Last name
3. Phone
4. Email

Then **one** custom question. One. Every extra field costs you leads.

The one question worth asking, mapped to `program_interest`:

> `What are you interested in?`
> Options matching your programs, plus "not sure yet".

If you run kids programs, that question can be: `Is this for you or your
child?`, mapped to `participant_is_child`. Either is defensible. Pick the one
your gym most needs to know.

### Higher intent versus more volume

Meta usually offers a more-volume or higher-intent form version. Higher intent
adds a confirmation step.

**Choose higher intent.** Fewer leads, and they are meaningfully better. With
an agent following up in under a minute you do not need volume, you need
people who meant it.

### The thank you screen

Set it to say exactly what happens next:

> `Got it. We'll text you in the next couple of minutes with two times you can
> come in.`

Sets the expectation, and makes the agent's text expected rather than
intrusive.

### Consent

Your privacy policy link is required. Make sure the consent language covers
being contacted by text, which ties back to your A2P registration in 01.2.

## Before you publish

Check all five:

- [ ] Member list excluded
- [ ] Radius is tight
- [ ] Lead form has four fields plus one question
- [ ] Thank you screen says a text is coming
- [ ] Daily budget, not lifetime

## After you publish

**Test the lead flow yourself before spending real money on it.** See 10.4.

Then leave it alone for a week. Do not edit it daily. See 10.5.

## When it goes wrong

**Rejected by Meta.** Read the reason. Common ones for gyms: implying
personal attributes like weight or health condition, before and after images
that overpromise, or unrealistic claims. Fix the specific thing, resubmit.

**High cost per lead.** Radius too wide, offer too weak, or creative is stock.
In that order.

**Leads but no bookings.** The problem is downstream, not in the ad. Read what
the agent said. See 10.4.

## Video script

**Hook.** Ten minutes and you will have a campaign running. The part that
decides whether it works is the lead form, and it is the part everybody
rushes.

**Beats.**
1. On screen: create the campaign. Objective, name, audience. Steady.
2. On screen: write the ad copy live. Read it out loud. Then read a bad
   version out loud. The contrast teaches it.
3. On screen: the lead form. Slow right down. Four fields, one question.
   Delete a field on camera and say what it is worth.
4. On screen: higher intent versus more volume. Choose higher intent, explain
   why in one line.
5. On screen: the thank you screen. Say why telling them a text is coming
   raises answer rates.
6. On screen: the five-point pre-publish check.

**Go do.** Build the campaign but do not publish. Test the lead flow first,
in 10.4.

- Whether custom question mapping to a CRM field is available at build time
  or must be mapped after.
