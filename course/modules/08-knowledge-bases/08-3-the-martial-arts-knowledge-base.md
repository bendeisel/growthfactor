## Why this matters

An MMA gym gets asked completely different questions from a strength gym.

"Will I get hit on my first day." "How long until blue belt." "Is it safe for
my seven year old." "Do I need a gi." "Who is your professor under."

An agent running the general gym template will fumble every one of those, and
fumbling them loses the enquiry, because they are not casual questions. They
are the thing the person actually wanted to know.

## Section 3: Programs, per discipline

Per discipline, and two fields matter more than the rest.

**Contact level.** `[NONE / LIGHT / FULL, AND FROM WHEN]`

This is the single most common worry from a new adult and from every parent.
Answer it plainly and early. An agent that dodges it confirms the fear.

**Beginner path.** Is there a fundamentals class, or do beginners join the
main class? People want to know whether they will be thrown in.

Also: what it actually is, for someone who has only seen it on TV. Most people
enquiring about BJJ have watched a UFC clip and think it is that.

**Gear.** What you lend, what they must buy, when, and roughly what it costs.
Gear cost is a hidden objection. Somebody quotes $130 a month and then finds
out they need a $120 gi, and they feel misled.

## Section 4: Ranks and progression

The general template has nothing for this, and it is half of what people ask.

- Ranking system, and the order of ranks
- How long each typically takes, **honestly**
- How gradings work: test, coach decision, or attendance
- Grading fees
- Do you accept rank from another school
- Lineage or affiliation

**On rank timelines:** be honest. "About two years to blue for someone
training three times a week" is a good answer. Anything that sounds like a
promise is a problem, and the agent prompt forbids predicting a rank date for
an individual.

**On lineage:** martial artists ask, and getting it wrong reads as fake. Write
the association, the professor, the lineage. If you are independent, say so
plainly, there is no shame in it and pretending is worse.

## Section 5: Sparring and safety

Its own section, because it is the fear underneath most enquiries.

- When a new person first spars
- Is sparring optional
- Injury rate, and what you do about it
- Supervision ratio
- Rules for training with an injury
- Hygiene: nails, mat cleaning, skin checks, no shoes on mats, ringworm and
  staph policy
- What tapping is, in one sentence

Include the hygiene stuff. It sounds unglamorous and it reassures exactly the
kind of careful person who becomes a long-term member.

## Section 6: Kids program

Parents ask a different set of questions and they ask a lot of them. Give them
their own section.

- Age brackets and how classes split
- Is it contact, by age
- Discipline and behaviour approach
- Do parents watch
- Kids trial offer
- What a child wears to a first class
- Bullying and confidence: **what you actually do**, not a slogan
- Sibling rates
- Is competition required for kids

**The bullying question deserves a real answer.** A lot of kids enquiries come
from a parent whose child is having a hard time at school. A slogan about
confidence reads as marketing. A specific description of how a nervous kid is
introduced to the class reads as somebody who has done this before.

## Section 9: FAQ, the martial arts set

The template lists sixteen. The ones that matter most:

- Will I get hit on my first day?
- I have never done this, will everyone else be better than me?
- Do I need a gi, and when?
- How long does it take to get a blue belt?
- Do I have to compete?
- Do I have to spar?
- Is it safe for my child?
- Can I train if I am 40 / 50 / overweight / recovering from something?
- Do you have a women's only class?
- Do you accept my rank from my old school?
- What is the difference between gi and no-gi?

## Section 10 addendum: extra rules

Add these to the behaviour contract.

**Never:**

- Compare the gym to a named competitor school. This community is small and it
  always gets screenshotted.
- Comment on another school's lineage, credentials or quality.
- Predict how fast somebody will rank up.
- Tell a parent their child will be able to defend themselves by a date.
- Give any advice about training around an injury.

**Always:**

- Treat "will I get hurt" and "is it safe for my kid" as the real question
  behind most enquiries, and answer it directly rather than pivoting to the
  offer.
- Ask whether the enquiry is for an adult or a child before anything else.

## The competitor rule, specifically

Worth understanding why it is absolute.

Local martial arts communities are small, everybody trains with everybody
eventually, and a screenshot of your gym's bot criticising another school will
reach that school within an hour. There is no version of that which ends well.

The agent may say what you do. It never says what anyone else does badly.

## Test it

Ask the agent these five and read the answers carefully.

1. "will i get hurt"
2. "is this safe for my 7 year old"
3. "how long to get a blue belt"
4. "are you better than [named local school]"
5. "i'm 45 and out of shape, is this stupid"

Question 4 must be deflected without criticising anybody. Question 5 must be
warm and specific, not a sales pitch.

If any of the five gets a generic answer, that section is thin.

## When it goes wrong

**The agent oversells the kids program.** Section 6 is thin, or the tone of
your own writing is markety. Write it the way you would say it to a parent at
the door.

**It gets defensive about a competitor.** Section 10 addendum is not in there.

**It promises a belt timeline.** Rewrite the rank section to give ranges tied
to training frequency, and confirm the never-predict rule is in place.
