# Onboarding & Delivery

The original blueprint stops at the sale. That's the most expensive gap in it: at 14-month assumed tenure, **every month of retention is worth more than a month of prospecting** (`00-strategy/03-unit-economics.md §2`), and retention is decided in the first 30 days.

Onboarding is also the capacity constraint — **11 hours per client** under AI-assisted delivery ([`20-ai-delivery-stack.md`](20-ai-delivery-stack.md)), which is what makes 4 new clients/month possible for a single delivery FTE holding ~10 accounts.

> **The timeline below is the traditional-build schedule (24 days to live).** Under AI-assisted production the build stages compress hard and **client access becomes the entire critical path** — target is 10–12 days. Keep this version until three builds have been measured, then revise the dates rather than guessing at them. The *sequence* doesn't change; the gaps between milestones do.

---

## 1. The 30-day plan

| Day | Milestone | Owner | Risk if late |
|---|---|---|---|
| 0 | Welcome email + access checklist + kickoff booked | Sales | Momentum lost immediately |
| 1–3 | **Access granted** | Client | **#1 cause of delayed launches** |
| 3 | Kickoff call, 45 min | Delivery | |
| 4 | Tracking audit + baseline snapshot | Delivery | No baseline = no provable result at day 90 |
| 5 | Content & asset collection | Client | Site build stalls |
| 7 | Site structure + wireframe approved | Delivery | |
| 10 | Keyword + audience research delivered | Delivery | |
| 14 | **Site preview shared** | Delivery | First visible proof — protect this date |
| 17 | Site revisions | Client | |
| 20 | **Site live**, tracking verified | Delivery | |
| 21 | Campaigns built, in review | Delivery | |
| 24 | **Ads live** | Delivery | Guarantee clock is running regardless |
| 30 | First report + strategy call | Delivery | |

**Time-to-live target: 24 days traditional, 10–12 AI-assisted. Alert at 35 either way.** Every day past 30 measurably increases churn risk — the client is paying and has nothing to look at, and that feeling is what they'll remember at renewal.

Under AI-assisted delivery, **almost every day on this timeline is waiting on the client, not on us.** That makes access-collection friction the highest-value operational investment available: one-click access request links, a 15-minute screen-share slot offered at booking to collect access live, and a day-2 phone call rather than a day-3 email. See [`20-ai-delivery-stack.md §8`](20-ai-delivery-stack.md#8-where-the-constraint-moves).

## 2. Access checklist — sent day 0

The #1 launch delay, every time. Make it one message, one list, with a deadline and a reason.

```
Subject: {{company}} — everything I need to get started

{{first_name}} — welcome aboard. To hit our three-week launch I need
these by Friday. Most take under five minutes:

WEBSITE
□ Domain registrar login (or add us as a user)
□ Current hosting login
□ Current site admin login

GOOGLE
□ Google Business Profile — add ben@growth-factor.ai as Manager
□ Google Analytics — add as Admin (or tell me you don't have it)
□ Google Ads — accept the link request I'm sending
□ Google Search Console — add as Owner

META
□ Add Growth Factor as a Partner on your Business Manager
  (I'll send instructions — takes 2 min)

BUSINESS
□ Logo files
□ Photos — see the 30-minute checklist below. This one matters more
  than you'd think; real photos of your crew and your work beat
  anything we could buy or generate
□ Services list with pricing ranges (if you're comfortable)
□ Service area — which counties/zips
□ Where should leads go? (email, phone, CRM)
□ Who answers the phone, and what hours?

I've booked us for {{kickoff}}. Anything you can't find, don't hunt —
just flag it and I'll walk you through it on the call.
```

**Day 3 nudge if incomplete. Day 5 phone call.** Do not let this drift silently — a client who hasn't granted access by day 7 is already a churn risk and usually doesn't know it. Under AI-assisted delivery this is *the* schedule risk, not one of several: move the nudge to day 2 and make it a call.

### Photo checklist — sent day 0, alongside access

```
30 minutes on your phone, no photographer needed

□ 3 shots of your crew (working, not posed)
□ Your truck / van with the wrap visible
□ Storefront or office exterior
□ 5 completed jobs — wide shot + one detail each
□ Owner headshot, outdoors, natural light
□ Anything with your logo on it

Shoot horizontal. Good light beats a good camera.
Send them however's easiest — text is fine.
```

We do not generate or stock-source imagery representing a client's crew, work, or premises ([`20-ai-delivery-stack.md §7`](20-ai-delivery-stack.md#7--imagery--where-id-draw-a-hard-line)). Real photos convert better for local trust, and fabricated ones are deceptive regardless of intent.

## 3. Kickoff call — 45 minutes

| Minutes | Section |
|---|---|
| 0–5 | Confirm what we promised, out loud. **Read the handoff note back to them.** |
| 5–15 | Their business: services by margin, ideal job, jobs they don't want, seasonality |
| 15–25 | Their customers: how they decide, what they ask, what makes them choose a competitor |
| 25–35 | Their operations: who answers the phone, response time, what happens to a lead that doesn't book, existing CRM |
| 35–42 | The plan and the dates. Set expectations on the learning period |
| 42–45 | How we work: monthly call, how to reach us, what's included in the 2 hrs/mo |

**Two questions that predict the outcome more than anything else:**

1. *"When a lead comes in at 4pm on a Friday, what happens?"*
2. *"What's your close rate on the leads you get now?"*

If the honest answers are "nothing until Monday" and "I don't know," **say so now, plainly.** No targeting fixes a business that doesn't call people back. Naming it on day 3 makes it a shared problem to solve; discovering it in month three makes it an argument about whether we delivered. It's also the natural entry for the CRM/follow-up add-on.

## 4. Expectation setting — say all of this out loud

The most valuable five minutes of the kickoff:

- **Weeks 1–3 will feel quiet.** We're building. That's normal and it's not a warning sign.
- **The first 2–3 weeks of ads are a learning period.** Cost per lead starts high and comes down. Do not judge week one.
- **SEO is a 3–6 month curve.** Ads carry the first 90 days.
- **Lead quality beats lead volume.** If we send 40 junk leads we've failed even if the number looks good.
- **We need your feedback on lead quality** — mark the bad ones, it's how targeting improves.
- **You'll get one report and one call a month, and you can email anytime.**

Writing these into the kickoff recap email means they can be pointed at in month two, without friction, when someone gets nervous in week two.

## 5. Steady-state monthly cycle

| Week | Activity | Trad. hrs | AI-assisted |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | Report build + strategy call · previous month review | 1.5 | 1.25 |
| 2 | Campaign optimization: search terms, negatives, bid adjustments, budget shifts | 1.5 | 1.0 |
| 3 | Creative rotation, landing page or site tweaks, SEO work | 2.5 | 1.5 |
| 4 | Audience refresh (In-Market Match pull), technical checks, next-month plan | 2.0 | 1.25 |
| Ongoing | Site change requests (2 hrs included), lead-quality feedback loop, escalations | 2.5 | 1.75 |
| | **Total** | **10.0** | **6.75** |

**6.75 hours is the margin assumption in `00-strategy/03-unit-economics.md`** (62% gross margin). If accounts routinely run 10+ hours, that margin reverts to the traditional ~47%; at 13–14 hours it drops toward 30% and the business stops working. Track hours per account monthly and treat drift as a serious signal, not an accounting detail. The fix is almost always better templating, not working faster.

**Two line items that should not shrink**, whatever the tooling makes possible: the monthly strategy call and the lead-quality feedback loop. The first is retention, the second is what makes targeting improve. Compressing either trades a visible hour for an invisible cost — see [`20-ai-delivery-stack.md §4`](20-ai-delivery-stack.md#4-where-ai-does-not-go).

## 6. Monthly report — what goes in it

Every agency report has traffic and impressions. Ours leads with the number the client actually thinks about.

**Page 1, one screen:**
```
BOOKED JOBS FROM MARKETING        {{n}}     ({{prev}} last month)
QUALIFIED LEADS                   {{n}}
COST PER QUALIFIED LEAD           ${{n}}
COST PER BOOKED JOB               ${{n}}      ← the number that matters
TOTAL INVESTED (fee + media)      ${{n}}
ESTIMATED REVENUE FROM THOSE JOBS ${{n}}
```

**Page 2:** what we did, what we learned, what we're changing next month. Three short lists.

**Page 3:** channel detail, top terms, top pages, call recordings summary.

**Page 4 — the one nobody else sends:** *leads that didn't get a response within one business day.* Named, listed, with timestamps. It's uncomfortable, it's genuinely useful, and it's the single strongest retention asset available — it moves the conversation from "are the ads working" to "are we working the leads." It also sells the CRM/follow-up add-on without a pitch.

**Cost per booked job requires close-loop data from the client.** Ask for it at kickoff, make it a standing item on the monthly call. Some clients won't provide it; report cost per qualified lead for those and keep asking.

## 7. Retention

| When | Action |
|---|---|
| Day 30 | First report call. Set the tone: numbers first, no hiding a bad month |
| Day 60 | Quick check-in email. **"Anything not working?"** — invite the complaint before it becomes a decision |
| **Day 90** | **Guarantee review.** Formal, against the written definition. Then the referral ask |
| Monthly | Report + call, never skipped, even in a bad month |
| Day 150 | Renewal conversation, ahead of term end |
| Quarterly | Add-on review — what's the current constraint? |

**Churn warning signs** — any two, escalate to the founder within 48 hours:
- Missed two consecutive monthly calls
- Stopped responding to lead-quality requests
- Asked what's included / questioned an invoice
- Went quiet after a bad month
- A key contact changed
- Lead response time degraded (visible in call tracking before it's visible anywhere else)

**The save play:** call, don't email. *"I want to be straight with you — I don't think you're getting what you signed up for. Here's what I think is wrong and here's what I'd do about it."* Naming it first almost always beats waiting for the cancellation email, and it occasionally converts a churn into a longer commitment.

## 8. Add-on triggers

Sell from evidence, not from a calendar. Each of these appears in reporting on its own:

| Signal in the data | Add-on |
|---|---|
| Leads delivered, slow or no response | CRM & follow-up automation — $2,500 + $250/mo |
| Winning on both channels, budget-constrained | Third platform — $500/mo |
| Ranking is the ceiling, conversion is fine | SEO Accelerator — $1,500–3,000/mo |
| Creative fatigue visible in frequency/CTR | Creative refresh — $750/qtr |
| Campaign needs a dedicated destination | Landing page build — $1,200 |

`00-strategy/03-unit-economics.md §5` puts add-on attach at roughly the same blended value as a tier upgrade, at a fraction of the effort. **When the report shows the trigger, the add-on sells itself** — the pitch is just reading the data out loud.

## 9. Offboarding

Do it well. A client who leaves cleanly refers; a client who leaves angry costs more than they ever paid.

- [ ] Confirm in writing, honor the 30-day notice
- [ ] Full export: site files, database, content, all creative assets
- [ ] Transfer ownership of domain, GBP, Google Ads, Meta assets, GA4, Search Console
- [ ] **Disconnect their ad accounts from our tooling workspace** ([`20 §9`](20-ai-delivery-stack.md)) — we tell every prospect they own their accounts; that has to stay true the day they leave
- [ ] Final report with baseline vs. final
- [ ] **Exit conversation** — what would have kept them? Log it honestly
- [ ] Move to a light nurture list. `[ASSUMPTION]` a meaningful share of churned clients return within 12 months, usually after trying something cheaper

**We do not hold assets hostage, ever.** It's the right thing, it's a sales asset we use on every discovery call, and the moment we do it once we can no longer say it.
