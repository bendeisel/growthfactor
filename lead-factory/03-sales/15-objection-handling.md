# Objection Handling

`02-campaigns/11-phone-scripts.md §7` covers the fast versions for cold calls. This is the full library for discovery, proposal, and decision stages.

---

## The method

Most objections are handled badly because the rep answers the words instead of the concern. Four steps, in order, every time:

1. **Acknowledge without agreeing.** *"Yeah, that's fair."* Buys three seconds and lowers defenses.
2. **Diagnose before answering.** *"When you say too expensive — compared to what?"* Half of all objections resolve here, because the stated objection isn't the real one.
3. **Answer once, briefly.** Over-answering signals that you think they're right.
4. **Return to the close.** *"Does that change it for you?"*

**The most important habit:** treat the first objection as a symptom. "Too expensive" is almost never about money — it's about believing it won't work. Answering the price question with a price answer means answering the wrong question well.

---

## Category 1 — Price

### "It's too expensive."
> *"Compared to what?"*

Then branch on the answer:

- **vs. a freelancer at $500/mo** — *"That's real, and for some businesses that's the right call. The difference is what happens when it isn't working. A freelancer running your ads has no ability to change your site, so when the traffic converts badly, nothing gets fixed. You end up managing two vendors who each blame the other. You're paying us partly so there's nobody to blame but us."*
- **vs. nothing / current spend** — *"What's one {{service}} job worth to you? … So at $4,000 a job, this pays for itself at four extra jobs a year. If we can't clear four, we shouldn't be charging you — that's what the 90-day thing is for."*
- **vs. another agency at $2,500** — *"Then we're the cheap option. What's making you hesitate isn't price, is it?"*
- **Genuinely can't afford it** — Site & Search at $500/mo, honestly presented as a smaller scope. Or nurture with a date. **Do not discount Core to fit their budget** — a client who can't afford the fee also can't afford the media, and the engagement fails in month three.

### "Can you do it for $1,000?"
> *"I can't move the price, but I can move the risk. Three months instead of six, same rate — so if we're not working out you're out in 90 days instead of 180. That's the flexibility I've got."*

Concession ladder from `00-strategy/02-offer-architecture.md §3`. Never lead with price movement, and never move price without founder approval and a written revert date.

### "Why is there a term?"
> *"Because the thing we're selling doesn't produce in 30 days. SEO takes months, and ad campaigns need a few weeks of data before they're optimized. If you leave at day 45 you get a bad outcome and I get a bad reference — the term protects the result as much as the revenue. It's also why the guarantee is 90 days and not 30."*

### "Do you take a percentage of ad spend?"
> *"No — and honestly that's one of the main reasons to work with us. A percentage model means your agency makes more when you spend more, whether or not it's working. Flat fee means the only way we make more is if you grow enough to want more from us. You pay Google and Facebook directly, on your card. We never touch your budget."*

**This is our strongest single argument.** It's true, it's checkable, and it indicts a practice most of the market uses. Look for a chance to say it on every call.

## Category 2 — Trust and risk

### "We got burned by an agency before."
> *"What happened?"* — then be quiet. Let the whole story out.

Then: *"So the failure was {{their words}}. Here's specifically how we're set up differently: {{map to a real structural difference — account ownership, flat fee, one team, the guarantee}}. And you own everything, so if this goes badly you leave with your site, your domain, and your ad history. The last agency probably didn't offer that."*

**Do not rush this.** A burned prospect who tells the whole story becomes a good client; one who's talked past stays skeptical and churns at month seven.

### "How do I know this will work?"
> *"You don't, and I'm not going to pretend otherwise. What I can do is show you the data for your market before you spend anything, and structure it so the downside is capped — 90 days, and if you're not getting qualified leads we keep working free until you are. The realistic worst case is you spend a quarter finding out. The realistic best case changes your year."*

Honesty outperforms confidence here. A rep who claims certainty about a stranger's business is telling them they don't understand it.

### "You don't have case studies in my industry."
> *"Not yet. Here's my honest pitch on that: you'd be early, which means you get me personally instead of account manager number four. And I'd want your results badly enough that I'll over-invest to get them. If you'd rather have someone with fifteen {{vertical}} clients, that's a legitimate reason to go elsewhere and I won't argue with it."*

**Never fabricate a case study.** The first follow-up question exposes it, and the deal you save is worth less than the reputation you spend.

### "Can I talk to a current client?"
> *"Yes — let me ask them first. I don't hand out clients' numbers without checking."*

Then actually ask. Refusing to give references without permission is itself a trust signal.

## Category 3 — Fit and scope

### "I already have a website."
> *"Good — is it converting? Let me ask a different way: of the people who land on it from a Google search, what percentage call you? … If you don't know, that's the thing to fix first, and it's not a website problem, it's a tracking problem."*

If the site is genuinely good → Ads Engine at $1,000/mo. Qualify against the gate in `00-strategy/02-offer-architecture.md`. **Saying "your site is fine, you don't need the full package" is one of the most powerful trust moves available** and frequently converts to Core anyway.

### "I only want the ads / only the website."
Fine — those are real products. But diagnose first:
> *"Happy to. Before we do — the reason we bundle is that the two fail together. If I run ads into a site that loads in six seconds on a phone, I'm spending your money to send people to a page half of them never see. So: can I look at your site first and tell you honestly whether ads-only is a good idea?"*

This is the segment logic from `01-data/07-scoring-and-routing.md §2` in conversational form.

### "Do you work with [my competitor]?"
> *"I don't right now. And to be direct about it — I won't take a direct competitor of yours in the same county while we're working together. That's not standard in this industry and it costs me money, but I don't see how I'd serve either of you well."*

**Then honor it.** Write the exclusivity into the agreement, scoped to category + county. It's a genuine differentiator and it's cheap at our current size.

### "Can you guarantee me X leads a month?"
> *"No, and be careful with anyone who does — they either don't know your market or they're going to hand you junk leads to hit a number. What I'll guarantee is qualified leads in 90 days, defined in writing, and that we work free until you get them. That's a guarantee I can actually keep."*

Refusing a guarantee you can't keep sells better than making one you can't.

### "Can we do performance-based / pay per lead?"
> *"I've thought about it and the answer's no, for a reason that helps you: performance pricing makes me optimize for lead volume, not lead quality. You'd get a lot of form fills and no more booked jobs. Flat fee means I'm indifferent to volume and focused on whether your phone rings with the right people. Also, honestly, I can't fund your ad account and my payroll on the hope it works."*

## Category 4 — Timing and process

### "Not right now, we're slammed."
> *"That's actually the argument for doing it now — the setup takes about a month, so if you start when you're slow you're already behind. What's the timing you're thinking?"*

Get a specific date. → nurture with a `nurture_reentry_date`. **A "not now" without a date is a "no" that wastes future calendar space.**

### "I need to think about it."
> *"Of course. What's the part you want to think about — whether it works, or whether it's us?"*

The two answers need completely different responses. This question is the highest-leverage sentence in the whole document, because it converts a stall into a nameable objection.

### "Send me a proposal."
> *"Will do. Before I write it — what needs to be in it for you to say yes? I'd rather send you the right one than a generic one."*

Then book the decision call before hanging up. Always.

### "I need to talk to my partner/spouse/GM."
> *"Makes sense. Would it be easier if I walked them through it directly rather than you having to re-explain it? I'm happy to do 15 minutes with both of you."*

Never let a champion carry your pitch secondhand. They will do it badly, through no fault of their own.

## Category 5 — Data and privacy

### "How did you get my information?"
> *"Public business listings, plus search-intent data — it tells us which categories are heating up in which counties, not anything about you personally. Your company showed up in {{service}} in {{county}}. If you'd rather I didn't contact you again, just say so and you're off the list permanently, all channels."*

**Answer immediately, honestly, and offer the opt-out unprompted.** Every evasion here becomes a complaint. And note the precision — *category and county*, not individual. That distinction is both true and required (`00-strategy/05-compliance.md §5`).

### "Is that even legal?"
> *"Yes. Commercial email to a business is legal in the US as long as we identify ourselves, use a real address, and honor opt-outs — all of which we do. We don't cold text, because that one genuinely isn't clean. And we'll never sell or share your info."*

Volunteering the SMS distinction unprompted is disproportionately reassuring — it demonstrates that we know where the lines are, which is exactly what a prospect is trying to assess.

## When to stop

Not every deal should be saved. **Walk away** — and log the reason — when:

- They've named the same objection three times and none of the answers moved them. The objection is not the objection.
- They're demanding terms that break the model: rev-share, no term, no minimum media, a lead guarantee with a number we can't support.
- They're abusive, or they describe a previous vendor in a way that predicts how they'll describe us.
- The math genuinely doesn't work for them. Say so out loud: *"I don't think this is a good use of your money right now."*

**That last sentence, said honestly, generates more referrals than any close in this document.** The person you talked out of buying tells three people about it.
