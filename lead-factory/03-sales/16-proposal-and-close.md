# Proposal & Close

`[ASSUMPTION]` 45% of proposals sent become signed clients (`00-strategy/04-funnel-math.md §2`). That number is entirely determined by what happens in the 72 hours after the discovery call.

---

## 1. Rules

1. **Within 24 hours of discovery.** Enthusiasm decays faster than anything else in the process.
2. **Never send a proposal without a booked decision call.** A proposal read alone is a proposal misread. If they wouldn't book the call, the deal wasn't as warm as the rep thought — log that honestly.
3. **Always with a 2-minute Loom.** You lose control of the framing the moment they open the PDF by themselves.
4. **Their words, not ours.** The problem section quotes them verbatim from `pain_verbatim`.
5. **Two pages.** A long proposal at $1,500/mo signals that we're justifying rather than prescribing.
6. **One option, not three.** Tiered proposals cause deliberation, and deliberation causes delay. We prescribed on the call; the proposal documents the prescription.

**The last line is deliberately last.** It's the sentence that removes the final unspoken fear — being trapped — and it lands hardest at the end.

## 3. The Loom

Two minutes, screen share on the proposal, face on camera. Structure:

```
0:00  "Hey {{first_name}} — quick walkthrough so you're not reading
       this cold."
0:10  Read their own words back. "This is what you said, and it's
       what I built this around."
0:35  The four-step plan. One sentence each. Don't oversell.
1:10  Price, and immediately: "Ad spend is separate, on your card —
       I want to be really clear about that."
1:30  Guarantee, including the conditions. State the conditions
       yourself; don't make them find them.
1:50  "I've got us down for {{day}}. Come with questions or come
       ready to start — either's fine."
```

**Say the ad-spend exclusion out loud, in the video, every time.** It's the single most common source of late-stage deal collapse in this business model, and a rep who assumes it was understood from the PDF will be wrong roughly a third of the time.

## 4. The decision call — 15 minutes

```
"Did you get through the proposal?"
```
Then silence.

- **"Yes, looks good"** → *"Great — want me to send the agreement now?"* Don't re-sell. The most common way to lose a won deal is to keep pitching past the yes.
- **"I have questions"** → answer, then: *"Does that resolve it?"* → close.
- **"I need more time"** → *"Sure. What's the open question?"* Then get a specific date and a specific next step. Never leave with "I'll get back to you."
- **"We're going to pass"** → *"Appreciate you telling me. Can I ask what tipped it?"* Log the reason. Ask for a referral. Set the nurture date. **A clean loss is worth more than an ambiguous maybe** — it frees the calendar and it teaches us something.

## 5. Follow-up cadence — SEQ-PROPOSAL

| Day | Touch |
|---|---|
| 0 | Proposal + Loom + decision call booked |
| 2 | *"Any questions on {{specific section}}?"* — reference something real |
| 5 | New value, not a chase: a relevant proof asset, a competitor note, a seasonality flag |
| 9 | *"Where'd this land for you?"* One line. Direct |
| 14 | Breakup + nurture entry at 90 days |

**Day 5 is the one reps skip and it's the one that works.** Chasing lowers status; bringing something new raises it. A rep with nothing new to say should send nothing and move day 9 up.

## 6. Signature and handoff

**Required before the deal moves to Won:**
- [ ] Agreement signed (e-sign, countersigned same day)
- [ ] Payment method on file, autopay enabled
- [ ] First invoice sent
- [ ] Kickoff call booked **before ending the closing call** — not "we'll find time"
- [ ] Access checklist sent (`04-ops/17-onboarding-and-delivery.md`)
- [ ] CRM updated: won, source, self-reported source, discount applied, pain verbatim carried into the delivery brief

**The handoff note to delivery** — the thing that most determines whether month one goes well:

> **What they actually said their problem was** (verbatim) · **What we promised** (specific, including anything said on a call that isn't in the proposal) · **What they're anxious about** · **The guarantee number and start date** · **Their media budget** · **Anything a rep said that we now have to deliver on**

That last item is the one that matters. A promise made on a call and not written down becomes a broken promise in week four, and broken promises in month one are the leading cause of the churn that would blow up the tenure assumption in `00-strategy/03-unit-economics.md §2`.

## 7. Post-close

| When | What | Why |
|---|---|---|
| Same day | Personal welcome from the founder, not automated | Sets the tone for the relationship |
| Day 1 | Kickoff | Momentum |
| Day 30 | First report call | The first real proof point |
| **Day 90** | **Guarantee review** | Contractual, and the single highest-goodwill moment we get |
| Day 90 | **Referral ask, immediately after the review** | Right after we've demonstrably delivered is the only moment worth asking |
| Day 150 | Renewal conversation, before the term ends | Never let a term lapse silently into a cancellation |

**Day 90 is the most valuable moment in the entire lifecycle.** The guarantee has just been met, trust is at its peak, and the referral ask converts better than at any other point. `00-strategy/04-funnel-math.md §6` estimates referral CAC at ~$300 — a fifth of any other channel. Put it on the calendar as a task, not a hope.
