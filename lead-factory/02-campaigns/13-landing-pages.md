## 2. `/signal-report` — the lead magnet

**Single job:** capture a business name, category, county, and email in exchange for a real report. This is the top-of-funnel workhorse and the cheapest lead source we have.

```
┌────────────────────────────────────────────────────────┐
│  HERO                                                  │
│                                                        │
│  H1: See who's searching for your service              │
│      in your county — last 30 days.                    │
│                                                        │
│  Sub: We pull real in-market search data for Middle    │
│  Tennessee service businesses. How many people are     │
│  looking, who's advertising against you, and roughly   │
│  what they're paying per click.                        │
│                                                        │
│  Free. No call required.                               │
│                                                        │
│  [ Business name ] [ Category ▾ ] [ County ▾ ]         │
│  [ Work email ]                                        │
│  ☐ You can text me about this. Msg rates may apply.    │
│           [  Send my report  ]                          │
│                                                        │
│  Delivered within one business day, by a person.       │
└────────────────────────────────────────────────────────┘
```

**Below the fold, in order:**
1. **Sample report image** — the single most important element on the page. Show the actual deliverable, blurred where it needs to be. People convert on seeing the thing, not reading about it.
2. **"What's in it"** — 4 bullets: search volume by term, advertiser count and rough spend, seasonality curve, the 3 highest-opportunity terms in your category.
3. **"Why we do this free"** — *"We're a Nashville agency. This is how we find clients. It's more useful than another ad about growing your business, and you keep the report either way."* Naming the motive builds more trust than hiding it.
4. **Who it's for / not for** — self-qualification. Explicitly: *"Not useful if you're outside Middle Tennessee or spending under $1,000/mo on ads."*
5. **One-line footer CTA** to `/leads`.

**Conversion targets:** `[ASSUMPTION]` cold Meta traffic 20–30%, warm/email traffic 40%+. Below 15% means the sample-report image is missing or the form is too long.

**Form rule:** four fields, one checkbox. Phone is not required — requiring it cuts conversion sharply and we can ask on the reply.

**Fulfillment:** report delivered within one business day, **by a human, from a real inbox, as a reply that invites a response.** The follow-up is the point; the report is the permission.

## 3. `/leads` — the main offer page

Long-form. This page has to close, or at least book. Sections in order:

**Hero**
```
H1: Websites and ads that only chase people already looking.
Sub: One team builds the site, runs the ads, and owns the number that
     matters — booked jobs. $1,500/mo flat. You pay Google and Facebook
     directly.
CTA: [ Book a 20-minute walkthrough ]     [ Or get your county's data first ]
```
Dual CTA is deliberate: one high-commitment, one low. The secondary catches everyone not ready for a calendar, who would otherwise bounce.

**Section 2 — The problem, stated as theirs**
> *Either your ads work and your website leaks, or your website is fine and your ads are pointed at everybody. It's almost always one of the two — and when you hire separate people for each, neither one will tell you which.*

**Section 3 — What you get.** Two columns, unambiguous:

| Included in $1,500/mo | Not included |
|---|---|
| WordPress site — build, host, maintain, secure | **Ad spend. You pay Google/Meta directly on your card.** |
| On-page, technical, local & AI-search SEO | Additional platforms beyond two (+$500/mo ea.) |
| Two ad channels, built and managed | Video, photography, e-commerce |
| In-Market Match audience build + monthly refresh | Custom development |
| Call tracking, conversion tracking, lead inbox | |
| Monthly report + strategy call | |
| 2 hrs/mo of site changes | |

**The exclusions column is not a legal disclaimer — it's a sales asset.** Every prospect has been surprised by an invoice before. Showing what's *not* included, prominently, before they ask, does more for trust than any testimonial.

**Section 4 — How it works.** Week 1 discovery & access · Weeks 2–3 site build · Week 3 tracking & campaign build · Week 4 live · Day 30 first report · Day 90 guarantee review.

**Section 5 — The guarantee.** Full text including the qualified-lead definition and the client-side conditions. Stating the conditions plainly *increases* credibility — an unconditional guarantee reads as marketing, a specific one reads as a contract.

**Section 6 — Pricing.** All three tiers, visibly. Yes, publish it. Hidden pricing invites price-shopping and burns discovery calls on people who were never going to pay.

**Section 7 — Proof.** Case studies when they exist. Until then: the founder's story, the actual process, the client roster if permitted. **Do not fake this section.** An empty proof section is survivable; a discovered fabrication is not.

**Section 8 — FAQ.** Objections from `03-sales/15-objection-handling.md`, answered in their words: *Why not just hire a freelancer? · What if I already have a site? · Do I own everything? · What if it doesn't work? · Why a 6-month term? · How much should I spend on ads? · Do you work outside Nashville?*

**Section 9 — Final CTA.** Same dual structure as hero.

## 6. Universal requirements

Non-negotiable on every page. **We sell these things; our own pages have to demonstrate them.**

- **Mobile PageSpeed ≥ 85.** We open cold emails by criticizing prospects' scores. Ours being worse is an unrecoverable position.
- **Above-the-fold CTA on mobile**, no scroll required
- **Click-to-call in the header**, tracked
- **Forms: minimum viable fields.** Every field costs conversion
- **Thank-you page with a next step** — never a dead end. Book now, or watch a 2-min explainer
- **Tracking:** GA4, Meta pixel, Google Ads tag, call tracking with dynamic number insertion, form-submit and call-start conversion events, UTM capture into hidden form fields and passed to the CRM
- **Speed-to-lead automation** fires on every submission (`02-campaigns/10-sms-program.md §3 SMS-3`)
- **Privacy policy and terms linked in the footer** — required by ad platforms, and a review-time blocker if missing
- **Physical address in the footer** — CAN-SPAM, and it makes us real

## 7. Testing

Same discipline as email: one variable, enough volume to read it.

| Priority | Test | Hypothesis |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | `/leads` hero H1: outcome-led vs. mechanism-led | Outcome wins on cold, mechanism on warm |
| 2 | Dual CTA vs. single CTA | Dual captures more total, single books more meetings |
| 3 | Price visible vs. gated | Visible lowers volume, raises qualified rate |
| 4 | `/signal-report` with vs. without the sample image | Image should be decisive |
| 5 | 4-field vs. 3-field form | Each removed field ≈ +5–10% conversion |

Minimum 200 conversions per arm before calling a result. At our volumes that's several weeks — **resist reading a test at day four.** The most expensive mistake in conversion testing is acting on noise, and it's the easiest one to make when volume is low.
