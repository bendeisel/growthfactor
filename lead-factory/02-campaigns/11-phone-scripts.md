# Phone Scripts — Ring 1 & Ring 2

**Phone is our best channel and the original plan buried it at Stage 4.** At `[ASSUMPTION]` ~1.2% contact-to-client versus ~0.15% for email, a dial is worth roughly 8 emails. Its constraint is hours, not list size — which is exactly why it gets pointed only at Band A and B, Ring 1 and 2 (`01-data/07-scoring-and-routing.md §4`).

**Before every call block:** DNC scrub (federal + TN + internal), local calling hours 8am–9pm, identify yourself in the first sentence, no recording without announcing it.

---

## 1. The frame

We are not cold calling. We're calling a business that produced a signal we can name, in a county we work in, about a service we deliver. **That's a referral-adjacent call and should sound like one** — relaxed, specific, unhurried.

The three failure modes, in order of frequency: sounding like a script, asking for a meeting too early, and talking past the first "no" reflexively rather than actually listening to it.

## 2. Opener — Band A, `SWITCH` or `BOTH` intent

```
"Hey, is this {{first_name}}?

Great — Ben with Growth Factor here in Nashville. You don't know me, so
I'll be quick and you can tell me to get lost.

I pull search data for {{county}} — who's looking for {{service}}, who's
advertising against them. {{company}} came up.

Did I catch you at a terrible time?"
```

**"Did I catch you at a terrible time?"** — the permission close. It gives them an easy exit, which paradoxically produces more continuations than "do you have a minute?" People say no to *bad time* far more readily than yes to *a minute*, and the no-that-means-yes keeps the conversation.

Then, depending on the answer:

**"No, what's up?"** → go to §3.
**"Yeah, I'm busy."** → *"Totally fair. Worth 30 seconds later this week, or should I just email you the {{county}} numbers and leave you alone?"* — either outcome is a win; the email version gets an opt-in.
**"What are you selling?"** → *"Websites and ads for {{service}} companies, $1,500 a month. But I'm not going to pitch you on a cold call — I've got data on your market and I'd rather just send it."* Directness here consistently outperforms deflection.

## 3. The 30-second frame

```
"So here's the whole thing.

Most {{service}} companies around here have one of two problems. Either
the ads work and the website leaks, or the website is fine and the ads are
pointed at everybody instead of the people actually shopping.

We do both, one fee — $1,500 a month, you pay Google and Facebook directly
on your own card, so there's no markup and no games with your budget.

I don't know yet if that's relevant to you. Can I ask two questions?"
```

Then stop talking. **The two-question ask is the real close of the opener** — it converts a pitch into a conversation and hands them control.

## 4. The two questions

**Q1:** *"Right now, where do most of your jobs actually come from?"*
**Q2:** *"And if you wanted 20% more of them next quarter — what's the thing standing in the way?"*

These do all the qualifying work. Q1 surfaces channel dependency (usually referral, which caps growth and they know it). Q2 makes them name the constraint in their own words, which is the language we'll use for the rest of the relationship.

Listen for:
- **"Referrals, mostly"** → *"So no second channel. That's the thing that keeps most companies your size flat — one bad quarter of referrals and there's no backup."*
- **"We run some ads"** → *"Do you know what a booked job costs you? Most people know spend and revenue but nothing in between."*
- **"We tried an agency"** → highest-value answer available. *"What happened?"* Then be quiet for as long as it takes.

## 5. Transition to a meeting

Only after they've named a real constraint:

```
"Here's what I'd suggest. Give me 20 minutes on a screen share and I'll
walk you through the actual search data for {{service}} in {{county}} —
volume, who's bidding, what they're paying — plus a teardown of your site
and current ads.

You get that whether or not we work together. If it's useful and you want
help, we talk. If not, you've got the data.

I've got Thursday morning or Friday afternoon."
```

Two options, never an open calendar. **Never "when works for you?"** — it transfers the scheduling work to a busy person and produces "send me some times," which is where deals go to die.

## 6. Voicemail

Under 20 seconds. The **only** goal is to make the callback recognizable.

```
"{{first_name}}, Ben with Growth Factor in Nashville — 615-XXX-XXXX.

I pulled the search numbers for {{service}} in {{county}} and {{company}}
came up. Thought you'd want to see them.

Sending an email too so you're not chasing me. 615-XXX-XXXX."
```

**Always email within 5 minutes of leaving a voicemail**, referencing the call. The two together convert dramatically better than either alone, and the email gives them a low-friction way to respond.

## 7. Objections — the seven you'll actually hear

**"We already have someone."**
> *"Good — most people who don't are in worse shape. Curious, do you own the ad accounts or do they? … The reason I ask is that if it's theirs, you can't take the history with you, and that history is usually the most valuable thing in the account."*
> Not a rebuttal. A question that plants doubt they'll check on their own.

**"Send me some info."**
> *"Happy to. So it's not generic — what would actually be useful: the {{county}} search data, or a teardown of what you're running now?"*
> Converts a brush-off into a preference, and a preference into an opt-in.

**"$1,500 is too much."**
> *"Compared to what, out of curiosity?"* — then listen. If it's compared to a freelancer, the answer is scope. If to nothing, the answer is math: *"One {{service}} job is what, four grand? So this pays for itself at one extra job a quarter. If we can't do that, we shouldn't be charging you."*

**"We tried ads, they didn't work."**
> *"Yeah — what happened?"* Silence. Then: *"That's the most common version of it. Ads sending traffic to a site that wasn't built to convert. The ads get blamed because they're the line item you can see."*

**"I need to think about it."**
> *"Of course. What's the part you want to think about — whether it works, or whether it's us?"* Two different objections requiring two different answers, and this is the only way to find out which one you have.

**"Just email me your pricing."**
> *"$1,500 a month, six-month term, you pay the ad platforms directly. That's the whole thing — no proposal needed to tell you that. The part worth a conversation is whether it'll work for {{company}}, and I can't tell you that yet."*

**"How'd you get my number?"**
> *"Public business listing — you're listed as the contact for {{company}}. I use search-intent data to find companies in categories that are heating up in {{county}}, and yours came up. If you'd rather I didn't call again just say so and you're off my list permanently."*
> **Answer honestly, immediately, and offer the opt-out.** Every evasion here creates a complaint, and the honest answer is genuinely reassuring.

## 8. Call blocks

Calling degrades fast when it's interleaved with other work. Block it.

| | |
|---|---|
| **Best windows** | Tue–Thu, 8:00–9:30am and 4:00–5:30pm local. Owners are at a desk before crews roll and after they return |
| **Worst** | Mon before 10, Fri after 3, anything 11:30–1:30 |
| **Block length** | 90 minutes, no email, no Slack |
| **Volume** | ~50 dials/block, ~250/week per seller |
| **Attempts** | 6 over 12 days, varying time of day, then → email-only |
| **Log every call** | Outcome + reason code, same day. An unlogged call didn't happen |

## 9. After the call — every time, no exceptions

| Outcome | Action | SLA |
|---|---|---|
| Meeting booked | Calendar invite + confirmation email + SMS opt-in ask | 5 min |
| Interested, no meeting | Send the thing you promised. **The thing, not a pitch** | 1 hour |
| Not now | Log reason + **dated** re-entry, move to nurture | Same day |
| Not interested | Suppress from calling, keep in email unless they said stop | Same day |
| "Take me off your list" | **Internal DNC, permanent, all channels** | Immediately |
| No answer | Voicemail + email within 5 min | Same call |

## 10. Ring 2 variation

Same script, one substitution. Ring 2 loses the in-person credibility, so replace the local proximity implication with category specificity:

> *"Ben with Growth Factor — we're in Nashville, we work with {{service}} companies across Middle Tennessee."*

And on the close, offer video explicitly rather than leaving location ambiguous: *"20 minutes on a screen share"* — never imply a drive-by you won't make.

**Ring 3 is not called at all.** Not because it can't work, but because calling hours are worth more pointed at prospects where the geo story closes.
